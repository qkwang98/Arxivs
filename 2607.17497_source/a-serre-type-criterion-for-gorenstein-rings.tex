\title[A Serre-type criterion for \(n\)-Gorenstein rings]
{A Serre-type criterion for \(n\)-Gorenstein rings and its application  to Nakayama algebras}

\author{Dawei Shen}
\address{School of mathematics and statistics \\
         Henan University \\
         Kaifeng, Henan 475004 \\
         P. R. China}
\email{sdw12345@mail.ustc.edu.cn}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 16E10; Secondary 16G20}
\keywords{Auslander-Gorenstein ring, \(n\)-Gorenstein ring, Nakayama algebra}
%\dedicatory{}
%\commby{}
\date{\today}
\begin{abstract}
Let \(R\) be a left and right Noetherian ring.
We introduce a Serre-type condition \((G_n)\), 
formulated in terms of the first occurrence and 
the flat dimension of indecomposable injective modules,
and prove that \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein if and only if it satisfies \((G_n)\).
We then apply the criterion to Nakayama algebras via syzygy filtration.
It is shown that syzygy filtration preserves the Auslander-Gorenstein property 
and reflects it within the class of
\(2\)-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras.
Combined with a result of Kl\'asz, Kleinau and Marczinzik, 
this shows that simple modules of odd grade over Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras 
are regular in their grade, 
thereby settling their conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Auslander-Gorenstein rings form a noncommutative analog of 
finite dimensional Gorenstein rings \cite{FGR75}.
Let \(R\) be a left and right Noetherian ring
and let
\[
  0\longrightarrow I^0(R)\longrightarrow I^1(R)
  \longrightarrow\cdots
\]
be a minimal injective coresolution of \(R\). 
The ring \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein if
the flat dimension of  \(I^i(R)\) is at most \(i\) for every \(0\leq i<n\), and it is
Auslander-Gorenstein if these inequalities hold in all degrees and the left
and right selfinjective dimensions are finite.
An Auslander-Gorenstein ring \(R\) is called Auslander regular if it has finite global dimension.
Auslander regular rings generalize the class of finite-dimensional regular rings 
from commutative algebra.

Well-known families of Auslander-Gorenstein rings are Weyl algebras \cite{Vo00}, 
enveloping algebras of finite dimensional Lie algebras \cite{Vo00},
higher Auslander algebras \cite{Iya07}, 
and blocks of the BGG category \(\scO\) \cite{KMM21}.


In this paper, we focus on the Auslander-Gorenstein property for Artin algebras. 
Determining whether an Artin algebra is Auslander-Gorenstein appears 
to be a fairly hard problem.
For instance, it is proved in \cite{IM22} that the incidence algebra \(kL\) 
of a finite lattice \(L\) 
over a field \(k\) is Auslander-Gorenstein if and only if \(L\) is distributive.
In \cite{Kla25}, it is shown that a gentle algebra \(A\) satisfies the 
Auslander-Gorenstein condition precisely when every vertex of its quiver 
has equal in-degree and out-degree.
Furthermore, the problem of classifying all Auslander-Gorenstein monomial algebras 
is reduced to that of classifying all Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras.

Although this definition of \(n\)-Gorenstein condition is
phrased in terms of  an entire injective coresolution, 
the individual indecomposable injective summands carry enough information 
to detect the property.


Our first aim is to make this observation precise in a form analogous to a
Serre condition.  
For an indecomposable injective module \(I\), let
\(j_R(I)\) be the first degree in which \(I\) occurs in the minimal injective
coresolution of \(R\).  

For every nonnegative integer \(n\), we introduce the condition
\[
  j_R(I)\geq \min\{n,\fdim_R I\}
\]
for every indecomposable injective \(I\), and prove that it is equivalent to
\(R\) being \(n\)-Gorenstein.  
In the commutative Noetherian case this is
compatible with the local characterization of Fossum and Reiten
\cite{RF72}.  
For Artin algebras it becomes a criterion involving the grade
of each simple module and the projective dimension of its injective
envelope.


The second aim is to apply this criterion to syzygy filtration  for Nakayama
algebras. 
The construction associates with a Nakayama algebra \(A\) a
smaller Nakayama algebra \(\varepsilon(A)\) whose module category is
identified with a filtration subcategory containing all second syzygies \cite{Sen19}.  
For any \(n\geq 2\), we prove the  reduction formula
\[
  A\text{ is }n\text{-Gorenstein} 
  \iff
  A\text{ is }2\text{-Gorenstein and }\varepsilon(A)
  \text{ is }(n-2)\text{-Gorenstein}.
\]
It follows that syzygy filtration preserves the Auslander-Gorenstein property
and reflects it for $2$-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras; in the Auslander-Gorenstein
case, the self-injective dimension decreases by two.

Finally, let \(A\) be an Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra.  
We prove that, for every simple module \(S\) and every odd integer \(n\), 
the \(n\)-th Ext group  of \(S\) by \(A\) is either trivial or a 
simple right \(A\)-module. 
Over a split algebra, this implies the corresponding multiplicity-free statement
for the odd terms in the minimal injective coresolution of \(A\). 
Together with a result in \cite{KKM26}, our result
also shows that every simple module of odd grade is regular in its grade.
Over a split algebra, 
this establishes the dimension bound conjectured in \cite{KKM26}
by  Kl\'asz, Kleinau and Marczinzik.


The paper is organized as follows.
In Section~2, we introduce the Serre-type condition for Noetherian rings. 
Section~3 develops syzygy reduction techniques for Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras. 
In Section~4, we prove the dimension bound conjecture proposed by  Kl\'asz, Kleinau and Marczinzik.

Throughout this paper, all modules are left modules unless otherwise stated.


\section{Serre-type condition for Noetherian rings}
In this section, we introduce a  Serre-type condition for Noetherian rings, 
and prove that a Noetherian ring is \(n\)-Gorenstein if and only if this condition holds. 
We refer readers  to  \cite{Lam99} for foundational background  on rings and modules.


Let \(R\) be a left and right Noetherian ring. 
A submodule \(N\) of an \(R\)-module \(M\) is said to be {essential} 
if the intersection \(N\cap L\) is nonzero for every nonzero submodule \(L\) of \(M\).
Recall that the intersection of all essential submodules of \(M\) is 
precisely the sum of all simple submodules of \(M\).


For any \(R\)-module \(M\), an injective envelope \(I(M)\) of \(M\) 
is an injective \(R\)-module such that \(M\) is an essential submodule of \(I(M)\).
The injective envelope of any \(R\)-module \(M\) always exists and 
any two injective envelopes of \(M\) are isomorphic over \(M\).

Consider a minimal injective coresolution 
\begin{equation*}
    0 \to I^0(R)\overset{f^0}\lto I^1(R)\overset{f^1}\lto \cdots
\end{equation*}
of \(R\), 
where \(\Ker{f^n}\) is an essential submodule of \(I^n(R)\) for all \(n\).
For any indecomposable injective \(R\)-module  \(I\),
we let \(j_R(I)\) denote the minimal integer \(n\) (or \(\infty\)) such that 
\(I\) is a direct summand of \(I^n(R)\).


For $R$-modules $X$ and $Y$, we write
$\uHom_R(X,Y)$ for the quotient of $\Hom_R(X,Y)$ by the subgroup of maps
factoring through projective modules, and $\oHom_R(X,Y)$ for the quotient by
the subgroup of maps factoring through injective modules.

We have the following.

\begin{lem} \label{lem:j-grade}
Let \(S\) be a simple \(R\)-module. Then
\begin{equation*}
    j_R(I(S))=\grade_RS.
\end{equation*}
\end{lem}



\begin{proof}
    Take a minimal injective coresolution of \(R\) as follows
    \begin{equation*}
        0 \to I^0(R)\overset{f^0}\lto I^1(R)\overset{f^1}\lto \cdots.
    \end{equation*}
    For any \(n\ge 0\), the kernel of \(f^n\) is an essential submodule of \(I^n(R)\).
    Since \(S\) is simple, we have \(\Hom_R(S,f^n)=0\).
    There is an isomorphism
    \begin{equation*}
        \Ext^n_R(S,R)\cong \Hom_R(S,I^n(R)).
    \end{equation*}
    Note that \(\Hom_R(S,I^n(R))\) is nonzero if and only if \(I(S)\) is a direct summand of \(I^n(R)\).
    Then \(j_RI(S)\) and \(\grade_RS\) are equal.
\end{proof}



For $R$-modules $X$ and $Y$, we write
$\uHom_R(X,Y)$ for the quotient of $\Hom_R(X,Y)$ by the subgroup of maps
factoring through projective modules, and $\oHom_R(X,Y)$ for the quotient by
the subgroup of maps factoring through injective modules.


For a finitely generated \(R\)-module \(X\), 
it admits a projective presentation
\begin{equation*}
    P_1 \overset{g}\lto P_0 \to X \to 0.
\end{equation*}
The {transpose} \(\Tr X\) of \(X\)
is defined so that the sequence of \(R^{\op}\)-modules
\begin{equation*}
    0\from \Tr X \from P^*_1 \overset{g^*}\lfrom P^*_0
\end{equation*}
is exact, where \(P^* = \Hom_R(P,R)\).




We recall the following lemma; see  \cite[2.8]{AB69}.



\begin{lem} \label{lem:abformula}
Let \(X\) be a finitely generated \(R\)-module and \(C\) be an \(R\)-module. 
There is a natural isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
    \Tor^R_1(\Tr X,C)\cong \uHom_R(X,C).
\end{equation*}
\end{lem}




We need the following well known result; see \cite[VI.5.5]{ARS95}.


\begin{lem} \label{lem:pdim}
    Let \(X\) be a finitely generated \(R\)-module with \(\pdim_RX=g<\infty\). Then
    \begin{equation*}
        \Ext^g_R(X,R)\neq 0.
    \end{equation*}
\end{lem}


The proof of the following result is essentially contained in \cite[Lemma 3.2]{KMT25}. 
We drop the finiteness assumption on the grade.



\begin{prop} \label{prop:grade-ineq}
    Let \(C\) be an \(R\)-module and \(X\) be a  finitely generated submodule of \(C\). 
    Then 
    \begin{equation*}
        \grade_RX\le \fdim_RC.
    \end{equation*}
\end{prop}


\begin{proof}
Let us assume that \(\fdim_RC=g<\infty\) and \(\grade_RX>g\).
By Lemma \ref{lem:pdim}, we have \(\pdim_RX>g\).
We claim that there exists a finitely presented \(R^{\op}\)-module \(Y\) of projective dimension \(g+1\) 
such that \(\Tor_{g+1}^R(Y,X) \neq 0\).

Consider a projective resolution of \(M\) truncated at the \((g+1)\)-th term
\begin{equation*}
    P_{g+1}\overset{f_{g+1}}\lto P_g\to \cdots\to P_1\to P_0\to 0.
\end{equation*}
By the assumption \(\grade_RX>g\), we have
\(\Ext^i_R(X,R)=0\) for all \(0\le i\le g\). 
We apply \(\Hom_R(-,R)\) to this truncated resolution and obtain a projective resolution 
\begin{equation*}
    0\from P_{g+1}^*\overset{f_{g+1}^*}\lfrom P_g^*\from\cdots\from P_1^*\from P_0^*\from 0
\end{equation*}
of \(Y =\Tr\Omega^g(X)\), where \(\Omega^{g}(X)=\Coker f_{g+1}\).
By Lemma \ref{lem:abformula}, we have 
\begin{equation*}
    \Tor_{g+1}^R(Y,X)
    \cong\Tor_1^R(\Tr\Omega^g(X),\Omega^g(X))
    \cong \uHom_R(\Omega^g(X),\Omega^g(X)).
\end{equation*} 
Since $\pdim_RX>g$,  the module $\Omega^g(X)$ is non-projective.
Consequently, its identity map is nonzero in
$\uEnd_R(\Omega^g(X))$, so the last term is nonzero and
$\pdim_{R^{\op}}Y=g+1$.


Now let us  consider the following short exact sequence
of \(R\)-modules
\begin{equation*}
    0 \to X \overset{\mathrm{inc}}\lto C \to C/X \to 0,
\end{equation*}
where \(\mathrm{inc}\) denotes the natural inclusion map.
Applying \(Y\otimes_R-\) to this, 
we get a usual long exact  sequence of Abelian groups
\begin{equation*}
    \cdots\to \Tor_{g+2}^R(Y,C/X)\to \Tor_{g+1}^R(Y,X)\to \Tor_{g+1}^R(Y,C)\to \cdots.
\end{equation*}
Since \(\pdim_{R^{\op}}{Y}=g+1\) and \(\Tor_{g+1}^R(Y,X)\) is nonzero, 
we have that \(\Tor_{g+1}^R(Y,C)\) is nonzero.
This  contradicts the assumption that the flat dimension of \(C\) is equal to \(g\).
The conclusion thus holds.
\end{proof}








For any \(n\geq 0\), a left and right Noetherian ring 
\(R\) is called {\(n\)-Gorenstein} 
if the flat dimension of \(I^g(R)\) is at most \(g\) for all \(g<n\). 
A left and right Noetherian ring \(R\) is called {Auslander-Gorenstein} 
if \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein for all \(n\) and 
the left and right selfinjective dimensions of \(R\)  are finite. 
Furthermore, \(R\) is called {Auslander regular} 
if \(R\) is Auslander-Gorenstein and has finite global dimension.


The \(n\)-Gorenstein property is left-right symmetric; 
see \cite[Theorem 3.7]{FGR75}.


\begin{lem} \label{lem:left-right}
For any \(n\ge 0\), \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein if and only if \(R^{\op}\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein. 
\end{lem}



We assume that \(R\) is a commutative Noetherian ring.
Let \(\fkp\) be a prime ideal of \(R\) and
\(R_\fkp\) be the local ring of \(R\) at  \(\fkp\).
By \cite{Mat58}, the injective envelope \(I(R/\fkp)\) of the 
\(R\)-module \(R/\fkp\) is an indecomposable  \(R\)-module.
Furthermore, every indecomposable injective \(R\)-module is isomorphic to 
\(I(R/\fkp)\) for some prime ideal \(\fkp\)  of \(R\).

For any prime ideal \(\fkq\) of \(R\), there is an isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
    I(R/\fkp)_\fkq \cong
    \begin{cases}
        I(R/\fkp) & \textrm{if}\;\fkp\subseteq\fkq ,\\
        0 & \textrm{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
In particular, \(I(R/\fkp)\) is actually an \(R_\fkp\)-module.
Since the localization functor is exact, 
by Lemma \ref{lem:j-grade} we obtain
\begin{equation*}
    j_R(I(R/\fkp))=j_{R_\fkp}(I(R_\fkp/\fkp R_\fkp))
    =\grade_{R_\fkp}R_\fkp/\fkp R_\fkp=\depth R_\fkp,
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
    \fdim_R I(R/\fkp)=\sup\nolimits_{\fkp\subseteq\fkq}\fdim_{R_\fkq}I(R/\fkp)=\fdim_{R_\fkp}I(R_\fkp/\fkp R_\fkp).
\end{equation*}
Here, \(\depth R_\fkp\) denotes the depth of the  ring \(R_\fkp\).

A commutative Noetherian local ring \(R_\fkp\) is said to be 
Gorenstein if its selfinjective dimension  is finite.
It is known that a commutative Noetherian local ring \(R_\fkp\) is Gorenstein if 
and only if its depth and selfinjective dimension are equal.

We recall the following characterization of commutative \(n\)-Gorenstein rings \cite{RF72}.


\begin{lem} 
    Let \(R\) be a commutative Noetherian ring and \(n \ge 0\). 
    The following are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein;
        \item \(\depth R_\fkp\ge \min\{n,\fdim_{R}I(R/\fkp)\}\) 
        for every prime ideal \(\fkp\) of \(R\);
        \item  \(R_\fkp\) is Gorenstein for every prime ideal 
        \(\fkp\) of \(R\) with \(\depth R_\fkp<n\).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lem}


\begin{proof}
(1) \(\iff\) (3) 
The equivalence is established in \cite{RF72}.

(2) \(\iff\) (3)
Since \(R\) is a commutative Noetherian ring, we have
\begin{equation*}
    \idim_{R_{\fkp}}R_{\fkp}
    =\fdim_{R_\fkp}I(R_\fkp/\fkp R_\fkp).
\end{equation*}
By Proposition \ref{prop:grade-ineq}, we have 
\begin{equation*}
    \fdim_RI(R/\fkp)=\fdim_{R_\fkp}I(R_\fkp/\fkp R_\fkp)
    \ge\depth R_\fkp.
\end{equation*}
Recall that the equality holds if and only if \(R_{\fkp}\) is Gorenstein.
Then (2) and (3) are equivalent.
\end{proof}




Inspired by the above result, we introduce the following Serre-type condition.



\begin{defn}\label{def:serre-condition}
    Let \(R\) be a left and right Noetherian ring and \(n \ge 0\).
    We say that \(R\)  satisfies {\((G_n)\)} provided that
    \begin{equation*}
        j_R(I)\ge \min\{n,\fdim_RI\}
    \end{equation*} 
    for every indecomposable injective \(R\)-module \(I\). 
\end{defn}


\begin{rmk}
    If \(R\) is commutative or Artinian, 
    then \(j_R(I)\) coincides with the depth of a local ring 
    or  with the grade of a simple module.
    By Proposition \ref{prop:grade-ineq}, 
    the inequality 
    \[\fdim_RI\ge j_R(I)\] 
    holds for every indecomposable injective \(R\)-module \(I\).
    It is unknown whether the inequality holds in general 
    for left and right Noetherian rings.
    The notation $(G_n)$ used here should not be confused with other
    conditions denoted by $(G_n)$ in commutative algebra; throughout this paper
    it refers exclusively to Definition~\ref{def:serre-condition}.
\end{rmk}



We give a new characterization of \(n\)-Gorenstein rings.


\begin{thm} \label{thm:n-gor-ring}
    Let  \(R\) be a left and right Noetherian ring and \(n \ge 0\).
    Then \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein if and only if \(R\) satisfies \((G_n)\).
\end{thm}


\begin{proof}
    \(``\implies"\)
    Let \(I\) be an indecomposable injective \(R\)-module.
    If \(j_R(I)\ge n\), then the desired inequality is immediate. 
    If \(j_R(I)=g< n\), then \(I\) is a direct summand of \(I^g(R)\), 
    and thus
    \[\fdim_RI\leq \fdim_RI^g(R)\leq g=j_R(I).\]
    Then \(R\) satisfies \((G_n)\).

    \(``\impliedby"\)
    Since \(R\) is left Noetherian,
    every injective \(R\)-module  is a direct sum of 
    indecomposable injective \(R\)-modules.
    Fix \(g<n\) and let \(I\) be an indecomposable direct summand  of \(I^g(R)\).
    Then \(j_R(I)\le g<n\). 
    The inequality
    \[j_R(I)\geq \min\{n,\fdim_RI\}\]
    forces \(\fdim_RI< n\) and hence \(\fdim_RI\le g\).
    Consequently, \(\fdim_RI^g(R)\le g\).
    This holds for every \(g<n\).
    It follows that \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein.
\end{proof}


We have the following.


\begin{prop} \label{prop:all-gor-ring}
     Let  \(R\) be a left and right Noetherian ring.  
     The following statements are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \(R\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein for all \(n\);
        \item \(\fdim_RI\leq j_R(I)\) for every indecomposable injective \(R\)-module \(I\).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{prop}



We now focus our attention on Artin algebras.
Recall that an Artin algebra is a module finite algebra 
over a commutative Artinian ring.
We refer to \cite{ARS95} for basic background  on Artin algebras.


Let \(k\) be a commutative Artinian ring and \(E\) be a minimal 
injective cogenerator of \(k\)-modules.
Denote by \(D=\Hom_k(-,E)\) the standard duality.

Let \(A\) be an Artin algebra.
Then every indecomposable injective \(A\)-module is isomorphic to \(I(S)\) for some simple module \(S\).
By Lemma \ref{lem:j-grade} and Proposition \ref{prop:grade-ineq},
\begin{equation*}
    j_A(I(S))=\grade_AS \leq \pdim_AI(S)
\end{equation*}
holds for  every simple \(A\)-module \(S\).


By Theorem \ref{thm:n-gor-ring}, we have the following.

\begin{thm}  \label{thm:n-gor}
    Let  \(A\) be an Artin algebra and \(n \ge 0\).
    The following statements are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item \(A\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein;
    \item \(\grade_AS\geq\min\{n,\pdim_AI(S)\}\) for each simple $A$-module \(S\);
    \item \(\pdim_AI(S)=\grade_AS\) for each simple $A$-module with \(\grade_AS<n\).
\end{enumerate}  
\end{thm}






By Proposition \ref{prop:all-gor-ring}, we have the following.


\begin{prop} \label{prop:all-gor}
    Let \(A\) be an Artin algebra. 
    The following  are  equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \(A\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein for all \(n\);
        \item \(\pdim_AI(S)=\grade_AS\) for every simple \(A\)-module \(S\).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{prop}


Recall the following  result; see \cite[Corollary 5.5]{AR94}.

\begin{lem} \label{lem:gor}
    Let \(A\) be an Artin algebra which  is \(n\)-Gorenstein for all \(n\).
    Then \(\idim_AA\) is finite if and only if \(\idim_{A^{\op}}{A^{\op}}\) is finite.
\end{lem}

This gives a new proof of  \cite[Theorem 3.3]{KMT25}.
We note that Theorem \ref{thm:n-gor} 
can be regarded as a refinement of this result.

\begin{cor} \label{cor:aus-gor}
    Let \(A\) be an Artin algebra. The following are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \(A\) is Auslander-Gorenstein;
        \item \(\pdim_AI(S)=\grade_AS<\infty\) for every simple \(A\)-module \(S\).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{cor}

\section{Auslander-Gorenstein condition for Nakayama algebras}


This section is devoted to the study of Auslander-Gorenstein  Nakayama algebras.
We prove that the syzygy filtered algebra of every Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra 
is also Auslander-Gorenstein. 

Recall from \cite{ARS95} that a Nakayama algebra is an Artin algebra 
whose indecomposable modules are all uniserial.
The valued quiver of a connected Nakayama algebra is either linear or cyclic.


Throughout this section, 
we work with Nakayama algebras satisfying the following two properties:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(N1)] each connected component of the valued quiver is linear, 
  or the valued quiver is cyclic;
  \item[(N2)] the endomorphism algebras of simple \(A\)-modules are all isomorphic.
\end{enumerate}
All statements involving the syzygy filtered algebra $\varepsilon(A)$ are understood within this class; the construction
again produces a Nakayama algebra satisfying (N1) and (N2).


It is standard that a Nakayama algebra has dominant dimension at least \(1\);
or equivalently, 
the injective envelope of its regular module is projective; see \cite{ARS95}. 
Thus, every Nakayama algebra is \(1\)-Gorenstein. 


We now fix a cyclic translation \(\tau\) on the complete set  of simple \(A\)-modules. 
By construction,   \(\tau\) agrees with the Auslander-Reiten translation 
for non-projective simple \(A\)-modules and 
sends  each projective simple \(A\)-module to an injective simple \(A\)-module. 
Such a cyclic translation is uniquely determined, 
provided that the algebra \(A\) is connected.



Denote by \(S(1),S(2),\cdots,S(m)\) the complete set of simple \(A\)-modules.
They are ordered such that
\[\tau(S(i))=S(i+1)\] for all \(i<m\) and \(\tau S(m)=S(1)\). 
Let \(P(i) = P(S(i))\) denote the projective cover of \(S(i)\), 
and let \(c_i\) denote the composition length of \(P(i)\).
The tuple 
\[(c_1,c_2,\cdots,c_m)\]
is called a Kupisch series of \(A\).
We note that the Kupisch series satisfies  
\[c_i\leq c_{i+1}+1\]
for all \(i\).
For each \(1\leq \ell\leq c_i\), 
we write the interval $[i,i+\ell)$ 
for the indecomposable module \(P(i)/\rad^{\ell}P(i)\).


In \cite{Rin13}, 
Ringel introduced a map \(\gamma\) on the set of simple \(A\)-modules, 
defined by
\begin{equation*} 
    \gamma(S)=\tau\soc P(S).
\end{equation*}
By  \cite[A.6]{Rin21}, the map \(\gamma\) restricts to a  bijection 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bijection}
    \{S\mid \pdim_AS\neq 1\}\overset{\sim}\lto \{T\mid \idim_AT\neq 1\}.
\end{equation}

We need the following.

\begin{lem} \label{lem:pidim-1}
     Let \(S\) be a simple \(A\)-module.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \(\pdim_AS\neq 1\) if and only if \(P(\tau S)\) is injective;
        \item \(\idim_AS\neq 1\) if and only if \(I(\tau^{-1} S)\) is projective.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lem}


Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra. 
Fix a simple \(A\)-module  \(S\) and write \(T\) for \(\gamma(S)\).
Then the injective dimension of \(T\) is not equal to \(1\).
By \eqref{eq:bijection} there exists a unique simple \(A\)-module \(S^*\) 
with projective dimension  not equal to \(1\) such that \(\gamma(S^*)=T\).
The {base module} \(\nabla(S)\) is defined 
so that the sequence of \(A\)-modules 
\begin{equation*}
    0\to \nabla(S)\to P(\tau S^*)\to P(S^*)\to S^*\to 0
\end{equation*}
is exact. 
By Lemma \ref{lem:pidim-1}, 
the module \(P(\tau S^*)\) is  injective.


The top module of \(\nabla(S)\) is isomorphic to \(T\).
Note that the injective dimension of \(T\) is not equal to \(1\).
The base module \(\nabla(S)\) is the minimal submodule of \(P(\tau{S^*})\) 
whose top module has injective dimension not equal to \(1\).


By \cite{Sen19,Rin21}, the set of all base modules
\begin{equation*}
    \scB=\{\nabla(S)\mid \pdim_AS\neq 1\}
\end{equation*}
form a semibrick in the category \(\mod A\) of all finitely generated \(A\)-modules.
The filtration category \(\scE\) of \(\scB\) is 
an exact Abelian subcategory of \(\mod A\) which preserves projective covers.
Moreover, the subcategory \(\scE\) contains the second minimal syzygy 
of any finitely generated \(A\)-module.


Denote by \(\scP\) the set of projective covers of all base modules.
The additive closure  of \(\scP\) coincides 
exactly with the projective objects in \(\scE \).
We denote by \(A_\varepsilon\) the direct sum of all modules in \(\scP\).

\begin{lem}\label{lem:ext-comparison}
For all $X,Y\in\scE$ and all $n\geq 0$, the inclusion
$\scE\subseteq\mod A$ induces a natural isomorphism
\[
  \Ext^n_{\scE}(X,Y)\cong \Ext^n_A(X,Y).
\]
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
The category $\scE$ has enough projective objects, 
and its projective objects are precisely
the modules in $\add A_\varepsilon$. In particular, every projective object of
$\scE$ is projective as an $A$-module. A projective resolution of $X$ in $\scE$
is therefore also an $A$-projective resolution. 

Since $\scE$ is a full subcategory
of $\mod A$, applying $\Hom(-,Y)$ to this resolution computes both Ext groups and
gives the asserted natural isomorphism.
\end{proof}
The {syzygy filtered algebra} \(\varepsilon(A)\) of \(A\) is defined as 
the opposite endomorphism algebra of \(A_\varepsilon\), that is, 
\begin{equation*}
    \varepsilon(A)=\End_A^{\op}(A_\varepsilon)=\End_A^{\op}(\bigoplus\nolimits_{P\in\scP} P).
\end{equation*}
It follows that the filtration category \(\scE \) is equivalent to the category 
\(\mod\varepsilon(A)\) of finitely generated \(\varepsilon(A)\)-modules.
The algebra \(\varepsilon(A)\) is a Nakayama algebra of rank  at most that of \(A\).
Furthermore, \(\varepsilon(A)\) also satisfies (N1) and (N2).


The construction of base modules yields a surjective map from the set 
of simple \(A\)-modules onto the set of simple objects in \(\scE\) such that 
\begin{equation*}
    \nabla(\tau(S))=
    \begin{cases}
    \nabla(S)& \textrm{if}\; \pdim_AS=1, \\
    \tau_\scE (\nabla(S))  & \textrm{if}\; \pdim_AS\neq 1.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
This induces a cyclic translation \(\tau_\scE \) 
on the complete set of simple objects in \(\scE\),
which in turn gives rise to a reduction
\[(A,\tau)\to (\scE,\tau_\scE).\]
The Kupisch series of \(\varepsilon(A)\) 
can be explicitly computed from that of \(A\).





The syzygy filtration provides a reduction technique for Nakayama algebras.
It works quite well on the homological dimensions of Nakayama algebras, 
such as global dimension, selfinjective dimension, finitistic dimension, 
\(\varphi\)-dimension, and dominant dimension; see \cite{Sen19} for more details.



\begin{exam}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra with Kupisch series
\[ (3,3,3,3,3,2,1). \]
It admits \(5\) base modules 
\[\nabla(1)=[4,5),\;\nabla(2)=[5,6),\;\nabla(3)=[6,7),\;
\nabla(4)=[7,8),\;\nabla(7)=[1,4).\]
The Kupisch series of \(\varepsilon(A)\) is \((1,3,3,2,1)\).
\end{exam}

We need the following; see \cite{Rin21}






\begin{lem} \label{lem:madsen}
Let \(M\) be an indecomposable \(A\)-module.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \(\pdim_AM=1\) if and only if every composition factor of \(M\) has projective dimension \(1\);
    \item \(\idim_AM=1\) if and only if every composition factor of \(M\) has injective dimension \(1\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lem}


Let \(S\) be a  simple module such that \(\pdim_AS\neq 1\).
By Lemma \ref{lem:madsen}, 
the projective dimension of \(I(S)\) is not equal to \(1\).
There exists a commutative diagram with exact rows as depicted below.
\begin{equation} \label{eq:comdiag}
\begin{tikzcd}
0\arrow[r] &\nabla(S)\arrow[r,]\arrow[d,] &P(\tau{S})\arrow[r]\arrow[d,"="] 
&P(S) \arrow[r]\arrow[d] & S \arrow[r]\arrow[d] &0\\
0\arrow[r] &J(S) \arrow[r] &P(\tau{S})\arrow[r] &PI(S) \arrow[r] &I(S)\arrow[r] &0
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}


The module \(J(S)\) is the maximal submodule of \(P(\tau{S})\) whose top module 
has injective dimension not equal to \(1\).
If \(I(S)\) is projective, then \(J(S)\) is an injective (and projective) \(A\)-module.
If the projective dimension of \(I(S)\) is at least \(2\), 
then \(J(S)\) is a non-injective \(A\)-module.
In both cases, \(J(S)\) is an injective envelope of \(\nabla(S)\) 
in the filtration category \(\scE\).
We obtain that
\[\scJ=\{J(S)\mid \pdim_AS\neq 1\}\]
is a complete set of indecomposable injective objects in \(\scE\); 
see \cite[Lemma 3.3]{She26}.

We have the following.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:injdim}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra and \(S\) be a  simple module 
such that \(\pdim_AS\neq 1\). 
Then
\begin{equation*}
    \pdim_{\scE}J(S)=\max\{\pdim_AI(S)-2,0\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma \ref{lem:madsen},  \(\pdim_AI(S)\neq 1\).
If \(I(S)\) is projective, then  
\(J(S)\) is  projective.
If \(\pdim_AI(S)\ge 2\),  it follows from \eqref{eq:comdiag} that 
\[\pdim_{A}J(S)=\pdim_AI(S)-2.\]
As the exact embedding \(\scE\subseteq \mod A\)  preserves projective covers, 
we have
\[\pdim_\scE J(S)=\pdim_AJ(S).\] 
Therefore, the conclusion follows.
\end{proof}

We need the following characterization of \(2\)-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras.


\begin{lem} \label{lem:2-gor}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra. The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein;
    \item \(A\) is \(3\)-Gorenstein;
    \item \(\pdim_AI(S)\le 1\) for every simple module \(S\) with \(\pdim_AS=1\);
    \item \(\idim_AP(T)\le 1\) for every simple module \(T\) with \(\idim_AT=1\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lem}


\begin{proof}
    (1) \(\iff\) (2)
    This is a consequence of \cite[2.10]{FI93}.

    (1) \(\implies\) (3)
    Let \(S\) be a simple module \(S\) with \(\pdim_AS=1\).
    By Lemma \ref{lem:pidim-1}, 
    \(P(\tau S)\) is  non-injective.
    There is an exact sequence of \(A\)-modules
    \begin{equation*}
        0\to P(\tau S)\to IP(\tau S) \to I(S).
    \end{equation*}
    Since \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein, we have \(\pdim_AI(S)\le 1\).

    (3) \(\implies\) (1)
    Consider a minimal injective copresentation of \(A\) as follows
    \begin{equation*}
        0\to A\to I^0(A)\to I^1(A).
    \end{equation*}
    By Lemma \ref{lem:pidim-1},
    \(I^1(A)\) is a finite direct sum of modules \(I(S)\), 
    where \(S\) runs through all  simple \(A\)-modules of projective dimension \(1\).
    Since \(\pdim_AI(S)\le 1\), 
    it follows that \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein.


    (1) \(\iff\) (4) 
    This follows from Lemma \ref{lem:left-right} and the equivalence of (1) and (3).
\end{proof}



By Theorem \ref{thm:n-gor}, we have the following.


\begin{cor} \label{cor:s3}
If \(A\) is a \(2\)-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra, then 
    \[\pdim_AI(S)=\grade_AS\]
for every simple module \(S\) such that \(\grade_AS<3\).
\end{cor}


By Proposition \ref{prop:injdim}, we have the following.

\begin{cor} \label{cor:selfinjdim}
Let \(A\) be a \(2\)-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra.
Then the right selfinjective dimension of $A$ is finite if and only if the right
selfinjective dimension of $\varepsilon(A)$ is finite. More precisely,
\begin{equation*}
    \idim_{\varepsilon(A)^{\op}}\varepsilon(A)
    =\max\{\idim_{A^{\op}}A-2,0\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
The left $A$-module $D(A_A)$ is the direct sum of the indecomposable injective
left $A$-modules. Then
\[
  \idim_{A^{\op}}A=\pdim_A D(A_A)=\max_S\pdim_A I(S).
\]
Under the equivalence $\scE\simeq\mod\varepsilon(A)$, the modules $J(S)$,
with $\pdim_AS\neq1$, form a complete set of indecomposable injective objects.
For the omitted simple modules, the $2$-Gorenstein hypothesis gives
$\pdim_AI(S)\leq1$. Proposition~\ref{prop:injdim} therefore yields
\[
  \idim_{\varepsilon(A)^{\op}}\varepsilon(A)
  =\max_{\pdim_AS\neq1}\pdim_{\scE}J(S)
  =\max\{\idim_{A^{\op}}A-2,0\}.
\]
\end{proof}


\begin{exam}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra with Kupisch series 
\[(3,3,2,1).\]
There are exactly two simple modules with injective dimension \(1\), 
namely \(S(2)\) and \(S(3)\).
Their projective covers are
\[P(2)=[2,5) \text{ and }  P(3)=[3,5).\]
Note that \(P(2)\) is injective and \(\idim_AP(3)=2\).
By Lemma \ref{lem:2-gor}, the algebra \(A\) is not \(2\)-Gorenstein.
\end{exam}

We need the following key lemma.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:uniserial-observation}
Let \(A\) be a  Nakayama algebra.
Let \(\nabla\) be a base module and 
let \(T\) be a simple module with \(\idim_AT=1\).  
If \(\idim_AP(T)\le 1\), then
\[
  \oHom_A(P(T),D\Tr\nabla)=0.
\]\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
If \(P(T)\) is injective, 
every morphism from \(P(T)\)  factors through an injective module, 
and the assertion is immediate.
Suppose that \(\idim_AP(T)=1\). 
By Lemma~\ref{lem:madsen},
every composition factor of \(P(T)\) has injective dimension \(1\).

If \(\nabla\) is projective, then \(D \Tr\nabla = 0\). 
Otherwise, \(D \Tr\nabla\) is an indecomposable
uniserial module. Moreover,
\[\soc D\Tr \nabla(S)\cong \tau\soc \nabla(S)\cong\gamma(\tau S).\]
This simple module has injective dimension different from \(1\) 
by \eqref{eq:bijection}.
If a nonzero morphism from $P(T)$ to $D\Tr\nabla$ existed, its image
would be a nonzero submodule of the uniserial module $D\Tr\nabla$ and would
therefore contain its socle. Then $\soc D\Tr\nabla$ would be a composition factor
of $P(T)$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:madsen}. Hence,
\[\Hom_A(P(T),D\Tr\nabla)=0\] 
and the stable quotient is zero as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem} \label{lem:ext-van}
Let \(A\) be a \(2\)-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra.
Let \(\nabla\) be a base module and \(T\) be a simple \(A\)-module 
with \(\idim_AT=1\). Then 
\begin{equation*}
    \Ext_A^n(\nabla,P(T))= 0 \quad \text{for all } n\ge 1.
\end{equation*}
\end{lem}


\begin{proof}
Since \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein and \(\idim_AT=1\), 
by Lemma \ref{lem:2-gor}  \(\idim_AP(T)\leq 1\). 
It follows that
\begin{equation*}
    \Ext_A^n(\nabla,P(T)) = 0 
\end{equation*}
for any \(n\ge 2\).
By the Auslander-Reiten formula and Lemma~\ref{lem:uniserial-observation},
\begin{equation*}
    D\Ext_A^1(\nabla,P(T))\cong \oHom_A(P(T),D\Tr\nabla)=0.
\end{equation*}
Since the  duality functor $D$ is faithful, 
$\Ext_A^1(\nabla,P(T))$ is zero.
This gives the desired conclusion.
\end{proof}




The tops of the indecomposable summands of $A_\varepsilon$ are precisely the
simple modules of injective dimension different from one. Consequently, there is
a decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-decomposition}
  A=A_1\oplus A_\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $A_1$ is the direct sum of the indecomposable projective modules $P(T)$
with $\idim_AT=1$.



We have the following.

\begin{prop}  \label{prop:grade}
Let \(A\) be a \(2\)-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra 
and \(S\) a simple module  with  $\pdim_AS\neq 1$. Then 
\begin{equation*}
    \grade_{\scE}\nabla(S)=\max\{0,\grade_AS-2\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{prop}


\begin{proof}
If \(\grade_AS<3\), by Corollary \ref{cor:s3} we have
\begin{equation*}
    \pdim_AI(S)=\grade_AS\le 2.
\end{equation*}
Proposition~\ref{prop:injdim} then shows that $J(S)$ is projective in $\scE$.
Since $\nabla(S)$ embeds into its injective envelope $J(S)$ and $J(S)$ is a
summand of $A_\varepsilon$, we have
\[\Hom_{\scE}(\nabla(S),A_\varepsilon)\neq0.\] 
Then $\grade_{\scE}\nabla(S)=0$.

Assume now that $g=\grade_AS\geq3$. Then $\pdim_AI(S)\geq3$, so
$J(S)$ is non-projective by Proposition~\ref{prop:injdim}. Since
$\varepsilon(A)$ is a Nakayama algebra, it is $1$-Gorenstein. Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:n-gor} inside $\scE$ gives
$\grade_{\scE}\nabla(S)\geq1$.

For every $n\geq3$, dimension shifting along the defining exact sequence of
$\nabla(S)$ yields
\[
  \Ext_A^n(S,A)\cong\Ext_A^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A).
\]
Using the decomposition~\eqref{eq:A-decomposition} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ext-van}, we obtain
\[
  \Ext_A^n(S,A)
  \cong\Ext_A^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A_\varepsilon)
  \cong\Ext_{\scE}^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A_\varepsilon),
\]
where the last isomorphism follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:ext-comparison}.
The first nonzero Ext group on the left occurs in degree $g$, 
then the first
nonzero Ext group on the right occurs in degree $g-2$. 
Therefore,
$\grade_{\scE}\nabla(S)=g-2$.
\end{proof}



The following is the main result of this section.
We adopt the convention that every  algebra is 0-Gorenstein.


\begin{thm} \label{thm:nak-n-gor}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra and \(n\ge 2\). 
Then \(A\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein if and only if 
\(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein and \(\varepsilon(A)\) is \((n-2)\)-Gorenstein.
\end{thm}


\begin{proof}
\(``\implies"\)
Since \(A\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein, 
then \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein and 
\begin{equation*}
    \grade_AS\ge\min\{n,\pdim_AI(S)\}
\end{equation*}
for every simple \(A\)-module \(S\).
If \(\pdim_AS\neq 1\),
by Proposition \ref{prop:grade} and Proposition \ref{prop:injdim} we have
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
    \grade_{\scE}\nabla(S) &=\max\{\grade_AS-2,0\}\\
                        &\ge \max\{\min\{n-2,\pdim_AI(S)-2\},0\}\\
                        & =\min\{n-2,\max\{\pdim_AI(S)-2,0\}\}\\
                        & =\min\{n-2,\pdim_{\scE}J(S)\}
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Then the algebra \(\varepsilon(A)\) is \((n-2)\)-Gorenstein.


\(``\impliedby"\)
Let \(S\) be a simple \(A\)-module with \(\grade_AS< 2\). 
Since \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein, 
it follows from Theorem \ref{thm:n-gor} that
\begin{equation*}
    \grade_AS = \pdim_AI(S)\geq \min\{n,\pdim_AI(S)\}.
\end{equation*}


Let \(S\) be a simple \(A\)-module with \(\grade_AS\ge 2\).
Then $\pdim_AS\neq1$ and \(\pdim_AI(S)\ge 2\).
Since \(\varepsilon(A)\) is \((n-2)\)-Gorenstein, 
by Proposition \ref{prop:grade} and Proposition \ref{prop:injdim}, we have
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned} 
    \grade_AS &= \grade_{\scE}\nabla(S)+2\\
              &\ge\min\{n,\pdim_{\scE}J(S)+2\}\\
              &=\min\{n,\pdim_AI(S)\}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Then the algebra \(A\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein.
\end{proof}


Combining  with Lemma \ref{lem:2-gor}, we have the following.


\begin{cor}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra and \(n\ge 0\). 
Then \(A\) is \(n\)-Gorenstein if and only if 
\(\varepsilon^i(A)\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein for every \(0\le i< \lfloor n/2\rfloor\).
\end{cor}    


\begin{proof}
For $n=0,1$ the assertion follows from the convention for $0$-Gorenstein algebras
and the fact that every Nakayama algebra is $1$-Gorenstein. For $n\geq2$, iterate
Theorem~\ref{thm:nak-n-gor}. Lemma~\ref{lem:2-gor}, which identifies the
$2$-Gorenstein and $3$-Gorenstein conditions, handles the final odd step.
\end{proof}

Combining  with  Corollary \ref{cor:selfinjdim}, 
we obtain the following.

\begin{thm} \label{thm:nak-ausgor}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra.
Then \(A\) is Auslander-Gorenstein  if and only if 
\(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein and \(\varepsilon(A)\) is Auslander-Gorenstein.
In this case, writing $\operatorname{vdim}A$ for the common left and right
selfinjective dimension, one has
\begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{vdim}\varepsilon(A)=\max\{\operatorname{vdim}A-2,0\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{thm}


\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:nak-n-gor}, $A$ is $n$-Gorenstein for every $n$ if and only if
$A$ is $2$-Gorenstein and $\varepsilon(A)$ is $n$-Gorenstein for every $n$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:selfinjdim} shows that, under the $2$-Gorenstein hypothesis,
the right selfinjective dimension of $A$ is finite if and only if that of
$\varepsilon(A)$ is finite. Lemma~\ref{lem:gor} then gives the corresponding
finiteness on the left. This proves the equivalence.

For a left and right Noetherian ring whose two self-injective dimensions are finite,
the two dimensions are equal; see \cite{Zak69}. The displayed formula therefore
follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:selfinjdim}.
\end{proof}

For algebras of selfinjective dimension at least \(3\), we have the following.

\begin{cor} \label{prop:nak-d-ausgor}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra and \(d\geq 3\).
Then \(A\) is Auslander-Gorenstein of selfinjective dimension \(d\) if and only if 
\(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein and \(\varepsilon(A)\) is Auslander-Gorenstein of selfinjective dimension \(d-2\).
\end{cor}

Recall that the rank of \(\varepsilon(A)\) is at most the rank of \(A\).
The equality holds if and only if  \(A\) is selfinjective.
There is an integer \(r\) such that 
\(\varepsilon^r(A)\) is selfinjective.

We have the following.

\begin{cor}
A Nakayama algebra  \(A\)  is Auslander-Gorenstein if and only if
\(\varepsilon^n(A)\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein for every \(n\ge 0\).
\end{cor}   



\begin{exam}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra with Kupisch series 
\[(3,3,3,3,3,2,1).\]
There are exactly two simple modules with injective dimension \(1\), 
namely \(S(2)\) and \(S(3)\).
Their projective covers are
\[P(2)=[2,5) \text{ and }  P(3)=[3,6).\]
Note that \(P(2)\) and \(P(3)\) are injective.
By Lemma \ref{lem:2-gor}, the algebra \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein.

The Kupisch series of \(\varepsilon(A)\) is given by
\[(1,3,3,2,1).\]
Note that \(\varepsilon(A)\) is not \(2\)-Gorenstein.
It follows that \(A\) is not \(4\)-Gorenstein.

For any \(n\geq 1\), let \(A_n\) be a Nakayama algebra with Kupisch series 
\[((3)^{3n-1},2,1).\]
For $n\geq2$,  the Kupisch series of  \(\varepsilon(A_n)\) is given by
\[(1,(3)^{3n-4},2,1).\]
The case $n=1$ is the algebra in the preceding example and is treated separately.
One can show that \(A_n\) is \((2n-2)\)-Gorenstein, but not \(2n\)-Gorenstein.
\end{exam}


\begin{exam}
Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra with Kupisch series 
\[(3,2,2,1).\]
There are exactly two simple modules with injective dimension \(1\), 
namely \(S(2)\) and \(S(3)\).
Their projective covers are
\[P(2)=[2,4) \text{ and }  P(3)=[3,5).\]
Note that \(P(3)\) is injective and \(\idim_AP(2)=1\).
By Lemma \ref{lem:2-gor}, the algebra \(A\) is \(2\)-Gorenstein.


One can show that  \(\gldim A=2\)  and 
\(\varepsilon(A)\) is semisimple.
Then \(A\) is Auslander regular of global dimension \(2\).
\end{exam}


\section{Regularity of simple modules for Nakayama algebras}

In this section,  we provide an affirmative answer to a conjecture 
raised by Kl\'asz, Kleinau and Marczinzik; see \cite[5]{KKM26}.



\begin{conj} \label{conj:kkm}
Let \(A\) be an Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra.
For every simple \(A\)-module \(S\) and every odd integer \(n\ge 1\), 
the \(A^{\op}\)-module
\[\Ext_A^n(S,A)\] 
is either zero or simple.
\end{conj}

We subsequently provide a rigorous proof of Conjecture \ref{conj:kkm}. 
To this end, we first establish several auxiliary lemmas.


\begin{lem}\label{lem:simple}
Let \(A\) be an Artin algebra.
Let \(M,N\) be \(A\)-modules with \(N=N_0\oplus N_1\) and let  \(n\ge 1\).
Suppose that  \(\Ext^n_A(M,N_0)\) is zero and  \(\Ext^n_A(M,N_1)\) is a simple \(\End_A(N_1)\)-module.
Then \(\Ext^n_A(M,N)\) is a simple  \(\End_A(N)\)-module.
\end{lem}


\begin{proof}
Additivity in the second variable gives
\[
  \Ext_A^n(M,N)\cong
  \Ext_A^n(M,N_0)\oplus\Ext_A^n(M,N_1)
  \cong \Ext_A^n(M,N_1).
\]
Let $e_1$ be in $\End_A(N)$  the idempotent corresponding to the summand $N_1$.
The corner algebra $e_1\End_A(N)e_1$ is naturally isomorphic to
$\End_A(N_1)$, and its action on the displayed Ext group is the given
$\End_A(N_1)$-action. 
Hence, every nonzero element generates the whole Ext
group under the corner algebra, and therefore also under $\End_A(N)$.
Then $\Ext_A^n(M,N)$ is a simple  $\End_A(N)$-module.
\end{proof}





Recall the following result; see \cite{Sen19}. 

\begin{lem} \label{lem:injdim1} 
    Let \(A\) be a Nakayama algebra of selfinjective dimension $1$. 
    Then \(A\) is hereditary.
\end{lem}


We now give a proof of Conjecture \ref{conj:kkm}.


\begin{proof}[Proof of Conjecture \ref{conj:kkm}]
Put \(d=\idim_AA\).  We argue by induction on \(d\).

We first treat \(n=1\).  If \(S\) is projective, then
\(\Ext_A^1(S,A)=0\).  Otherwise, Auslander-Reiten duality gives
\[
  D\Ext_A^1(S,A)\cong \oHom_A(A,D\Tr S).
\]
Since \(D\Tr S\) is simple and
\(\Hom_A(A,D\Tr  S)\cong D\Tr S\), the right-hand side is either zero or
simple.  By duality, the same is true of the \(A^{\op}\)-module
\(\Ext_A^1(S,A)\).

If \(d=0\), then \(A\) is selfinjective and
\(\Ext_A^n(S,A)=0\) for all \(n\geq1\).  If \(d=1\), Lemma
\ref{lem:injdim1} shows that \(A\) is hereditary, so only the case \(n=1\)
can be nonzero.  
Assume from now on that \(d\geq2\), and let \(n\geq3\) be odd.

If  \(\pdim_AS=1\), then \(\Ext_A^n(S,A)=0\).  
Suppose that \(\pdim_AS\neq 1\).  
Write
\[
  A=A_1\oplus A_\varepsilon,
\]
where \(A_1\) is the direct sum of the indecomposable projective modules \(P(T)\)
with \(\idim_AT=1\).  Dimension shifting along the defining sequence of
\(\nabla(S)\) gives an isomorphism of  \(A^{\op}\)-modules
\[
  \Ext_A^n(S,A)\cong\Ext_A^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A).
\]
By Lemma \ref{lem:ext-van},
\(\Ext_A^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A_1)=0\).  
By Lemma  \ref{lem:ext-comparison},
\[
  \Ext_A^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A_\varepsilon)
  \cong
  \Ext_\scE^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A_\varepsilon).
\]
Note that \(\nabla(S)\) is a simple object in \(\scE\).

By Theorem \ref{thm:nak-ausgor}, the algebra
\(\varepsilon(A)\) is Auslander-Gorenstein of selfinjective dimension \(d-2\).  
Since \(n-2\) is odd, the induction hypothesis shows that the last
Ext group is either zero or simple as a module over
\(\End_A(A_\varepsilon)\).  
If it is zero, then \(\Ext_A^n(S,A)=0\).  If it
is simple, Lemma~\ref{lem:simple}, applied to
\(A=A_1\oplus A_\varepsilon\), shows that
\(\Ext_A^{n-2}(\nabla(S),A)\), and hence \(\Ext_A^n(S,A)\), 
is simple as an \(A^{\op}\)-module.
\end{proof}

Let \(A\) be an Artin algebra and \(n\geq 0\).
Recall that a simple \(A\)-module \(S\) is called {\(n\)-perfect} if 
\begin{equation*}
    \grade_AS=n=\pdim_AS<\infty,
\end{equation*}
and \(S\) is called  {\(n\)-regular} if, 
in addition, $\Ext^n_A(S,A)$ is a simple \(A^{\op}\)-module.




Recall the following; see \cite[Proposition 5.2]{KKM26}.

\begin{prop} \label{prop:kkm}
    Let \(A\) be an Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra.
    Then all simple \(A\)-modules of odd grade are perfect in their grade. 
\end{prop}

By Proposition \ref{prop:kkm}, we have the following.

\begin{cor}
Let \(A\) be an Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra.
Then all simple \(A\)-modules of odd grade are regular in their grade. 
\end{cor}
