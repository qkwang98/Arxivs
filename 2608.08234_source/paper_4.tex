\begin{abstract}
Let $G$ be a simply connected simple complex algebraic group. It is proved by Oya, Qin, and Yakimov \cite{OQY} that the quantized coordinate ring $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ is generated
by generalized quantum minors, and therefore carries a quantized cluster algebra structure, for all $G$ but type $F_4$. In this article, we settle the $F_4$ case by an argument uniform across $G_2$, $F_4$, and $E_8$. The main idea is to bootstrap the existing proof in type $E_8$, which relies on Lusztig's canonical basis of the quantum adjoint representation, and replace it with the combinatorics of the quasi-minuscule
crystal. Consequently, $\mathcal{O}_q(F_4)$ also carries a quantized cluster algebra
structure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $G$ be a simply connected simple complex algebraic group. In the 
paper \cite{FZ}, Fomin and Zelevinsky conjectured that the coordinate rings of
various objects related to $G$ carry natural cluster algebra structures, and this
has since been verified in a wide range of cases. For the coordinate ring
$\mathbb{C}[G]$ itself, the expected statement is $
\mathbb{C}[G]=\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{U}$,
where $\mathscr{A}$ is the cluster algebra attached to the
Berenstein--Fomin--Zelevinsky seed of the open double Bruhat cell in $G$, and $\mathscr{U}$ is the corresponding upper cluster algebra. Here, we do not add the inverses of the frozen variables in the definitions of $\mathscr{A}$ and $\mathscr{U}$. This was established by Oya
\cite{Oya} for every $G$ but type $F_4$. The route passes through a generation
statement: asserting that $\mathbb{C}[G]$ is generated by generalized minors, the
equalities follow together with some other results. That generation property was known in types $A_n$, $B_n$, $C_n$,
$D_n$, $E_6$ and $E_7$, where $\g$ has non-trivial minuscule modules as tensor generators; the main
contribution of \cite{Oya} was to establish it in types $E_8$ and $G_2$, and
\cite[Remark~3.10]{Oya} explains why those techniques do not reach $F_4$.

The quantum picture runs parallel. Quantized cluster algebras were introduced by
Berenstein and Zelevinsky \cite{BZ}. In a very recent breakthrough paper, Oya, Qin, and Yakimov \cite{OQY} prove that
the quantized coordinate ring $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ is generated by generalized quantum
minors when $G$ is not of type $F_4$. Using this, combined with the quantum Laurent phenomenon \cite{BZ} and with the coincidence of
$\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ and the quantum upper cluster algebra established by Qin and
Yakimov \cite{QY}, one concludes that $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ admits a quantized cluster algebra
structure. Their treatment of the generation Theorem is again type-specific: $A_n$, $B_n$, $C_n$,
$D_n$, $E_6$, and $E_7$ are handled uniformly, type $G_2$ is settled by a direct brute force
computation \cite[Theorem~5.7]{OQY}, and type $E_8$ by combining Lusztig's canonical
basis of the quantum adjoint representation \cite{Lusztig} with $\hat{R}$-matrix techniques  
\cite[Theorem~5.9]{OQY}. Type $F_4$ is untouched again. In fact, except for the groups $F_4$ and $E_8$, they have proved this generation theorem over much smaller ground rings than $\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/2})$ (see remark \ref{srings}). 

Thus, before the present work, type
$F_4$ was the only explicitly left open case in both the classical and quantum settings. The purpose of this article is to settle the quantum $F_4$ case. Our main
result is the following.
\begin{theoremA}[$=$ Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, Corollary~\ref{cor:allG}]
Let $\g$ be of type $G_2$, $F_4$ or $E_8$ and let $V=V(\vp)$ be the quasi-minuscule
module. Then every matrix coefficient of $V$ involving the zero-weight space lies in
the subalgebra generated by those of non-zero weight. Consequently, for every simply
connected simple complex algebraic group $G$, the ring $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ is generated
by generalized quantum minors over $\mathbb{Q}(q)$.
\end{theoremA}
The three types treated together are precisely those for which $\g$ has no non-trivial minuscule modules, so
that the quasi-minuscule module $V(\vp)$ with $\vp$ the highest short root works as a tensor generator of the representation ring. The proof is uniform across the
three types, and therefore recovers the results of \cite[Theorems~5.7 and~5.9]{OQY} in
types $G_2$ and $E_8$ along the way. When $\g$ is simply-laced, $V(\vp)$ is the quantum adjoint representation, and the $E_8$ argument of
\cite{OQY} relies on Lusztig's description of the canonical basis for the quantum adjoint representation. In types $G_2$ and $F_4$, $V(\vp)$ is no longer the quantum adjoint representation. What survives is the crystal basis of $V(\vp)$, which
admits some common properties independent of the simply-laced condition. We study the crystal combinatorics of $B(\vp)\ot B(\vp)$  to locate the copy of $B(\vp)$
inside it and control the weights of both tensor factors at every node
(Proposition \ref{prop:twosided}), and lift these properties to the module level using the uniqueness of crystal bases (Theorem \ref{thm:Tminus}). This weight control, together with a braiding twist (Proposition \ref{prop:d}, Corollary \ref{cor:extract}), is exactly what is needed to run
the argument of \cite[Theorem~5.9]{OQY} in the remaining types. We immediately obtain the following Corollary.

\begin{corollaryB}[$=$ Corollary~\ref{cor:cluster}]
Let $G$ be of type $F_4$. Then $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ admits a quantized cluster algebra
structure over $\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/2})$.
\end{corollaryB}

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} fixes notation for the
quantized enveloping algebra, recalls crystal bases and the tensor product rule, and
introduces quantum coordinate rings and generalized quantum minors.
Section~\ref{sec:qm} describes the quasi-minuscule module $V(\vp)$, its crystal basis,
and focuses to the three types $G_2$, $F_4$, and $E_8$. Section~\ref{sec:main}
contains the crystal-theoretic heart of the argument: the two-sided weight control result and
its consequences for the projection $T\colon V\ot V\to V$, together with the
non-triviality of the braiding scalar $d$. Section~\ref{sec:minors} proves the main
theorem and deduces the two corollaries above. Finally, we conclude this article with a remark on why our present proof in the quantum setting fails to specialize at $q=1$, and note that the problem in the classical $F_4$ case remains open. 

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{The quantized enveloping algebra}\label{ss:qea}

Let $\g$ be a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra
$\mathfrak h$, simple roots $\Pi:=\{\alpha_j\}_{j\in I}$, root system $\Phi\subseteq\mathfrak
h^*$ and set of positive roots $\Phi_+$. We write $Q=\bigoplus_{j\in I}\mathbb
Z\alpha_j$ for the root lattice and $\Qp=\sum_{j\in I}\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\alpha_j$ for the
positive root lattice. Let $P$ denote the weight lattice, $P_+$ the dominant chamber,
$\{\vp_j\}_{j\in I}$ the fundamental weights, $\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_+}\alpha$
the Weyl vector, $W$ the Weyl group and $w_0\in W$ its longest element. We order $P$ by declaring $\nu\ge\nu'$ when
$\nu-\nu'\in\Qp$.

The unique $W$-invariant symmetric bilinear form $(\,,\,)$ on $\mathbb{R}.\Phi$ is normalised by
$(\alpha,\alpha)=2$ for short roots $\alpha$; we set $d_j=(\alpha_j,\alpha_j)/2$ and
$q_j=q^{d_j}$. We write $\alpha_j^\vee=2\alpha_j/(\alpha_j,\alpha_j)$ for the coroot of
$\alpha_j$ and
\[
\langle\lambda,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle
=\frac{2(\lambda,\alpha_j)}{(\alpha_j,\alpha_j)}\qquad(\lambda\in P),
\]
so that $(\lambda,\alpha_j)=d_j\langle\lambda,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle$.

Let $\K:=\mathbb Q(q)$ with $q$ transcendental, and let $\Uq$ be the quantized universal enveloping algebra (QUEA) of $\g$
over $\K$, generated by $E_j,F_j,K_j^{\pm1}$ $(j\in I)$. We shall assume its defining
relations are as given in chapter 4 of \cite{Jantzen}. $\Uq$ has a triangular
decomposition
\[
\Uq\ \cong\ U^-\ot U^0\ot U^+
\]
as a $\K$-vector space, where $U^+$ and $U^-$ are the subalgebras generated by the
$E_j$ and by the $F_j$ respectively, and $U^0$ is generated by the $K_j^{\pm1}$. Then
$U^\pm$ carries a $\Qp$-grading
\[
U^+=\bigoplus_{\eta\in\Qp}U^+_\eta,\qquad U^-=\bigoplus_{\eta\in\Qp}U^-_{-\eta},
\qquad E_j\in U^+_{\alpha_j},\quad F_j\in U^-_{-\alpha_j}.
\]
Finally, $\Uq$ carries the Hopf structure of \cite[Ch.~4]{Jantzen}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf}
\Delta(E_j)=E_j\ot1+K_j\ot E_j,\qquad
\Delta(F_j)=F_j\ot K_j^{-1}+1\ot F_j,\qquad
\Delta(K_j)=K_j\ot K_j,
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon(E_j)=\varepsilon(F_j)=0$, $\varepsilon(K_j)=1$,
$S(E_j)=-K_j^{-1}E_j$, $S(F_j)=-F_jK_j$ and $S(K_j)=K_j^{-1}$. A finite dimensional $\Uq$-module $M$ is of \emph{type $1$} if $M=\bigoplus_{\nu\in P}M_\nu$, where
\[
M_\nu:=\{\,u\in M\mid K_j\,u=q^{(\nu,\alpha_j)}u\ \text{ for all }j\in I\,\}.
\]
The graded pieces of $U^\pm$ then shift weights, $U^+_\eta M_\nu\subseteq M_{\nu+\eta}$
and $U^-_{-\eta}M_\nu\subseteq M_{\nu-\eta}$ for $\eta\in\Qp$. The dual $M^*$ is also equipped with a $\Uq$-module structure, defined using the antipode $S$
by $\langle x.\xi,u\rangle=\langle\xi,S(x)u\rangle$ for $x\in\Uq$, $\xi\in M^*$ and
$u\in M$. The category of finite-dimensional type-$1$
$\Uq$-modules is semisimple; its simple objects are the modules $V(\lambda)$ of highest
weight $\lambda\in P_+$. For each such $\lambda$, we fix a highest
weight vector $v_\lambda\in V(\lambda)_\lambda$. For each weight $\nu$ of $V(\lambda)$, we have
$\dim V(\lambda)_\nu=\dim V(\lambda)_{w\nu}$ for all $w\in W$. The weight space $V(\lambda)_{w\lambda}$ is called an extremal weight
space, and it is one-dimensional. Furthermore, the unique lowest weight of $V(\lambda)$ is
$w_0\lambda$.

\subsection{Crystal bases and the tensor product rule}\label{ss:crystal}

Let $A_0\subseteq\K$ denote the localization of $\mathbb Q[q]$ at the maximal ideal
$\langle q\rangle$, that is, the ring of rational functions in $q$ that are regular at
$q=0$. It is a local ring with maximal ideal $qA_0$ and residue field
$A_0/qA_0=\mathbb Q$, and
$A_0^\times=\{f\in A_0\mid f(0)\neq0\}$; in particular $1+qA_0\subseteq A_0^\times$. On a finite dimensional type-$1$ module the lower Kashiwara operators $\tilde e_j,\tilde f_j$
$(j\in I)$ act as in \cite{Kashiwara}. Fix $\lambda\in P_+$ together with the highest weight
vector $v_\lambda$, define
\[
L(\lambda):= \sum_{j_1,\dots,j_\ell\in I}
A_0\,\tilde f_{j_1}\cdots\tilde f_{j_\ell}\,v_\lambda\ \subseteq\ V(\lambda),
\]
\[
B(\lambda):=\big\{\,\tilde f_{j_1}\cdots\tilde f_{j_\ell}\,v_\lambda
\ \ \mathrm{mod}\ qL(\lambda)\ \big|\ j_1,\dots,j_\ell\in I\,\big\}
\ \smallsetminus\ \{0\}\ \subseteq\ L(\lambda)/qL(\lambda).
\]
By \cite{Kashiwara}, the pair $(L(\lambda),B(\lambda))$ is the \emph{lower crystal base}
of $V(\lambda)$. We call the elements of $B(\lambda)$
\emph{nodes} and each node $b$ being a shadow of a weight vector, we write $\wt b$ for its weight. The crystal $B(\lambda)$ is connected. Since $\g$ is of finite type, $B(\lambda)$
has a unique lowest weight node $b_{w_0\lambda}$ annihilated by all the $\tilde f_j$, it is of
weight $w_0\lambda$, and every node of $B(\lambda)$ is reachable from
$b_{w_0\lambda}$ using the operators $\tilde e_j$ alone: $B(\lambda)=\big\{\,\tilde e_{j_1}\cdots\tilde e_{j_\ell}\,b_{w_0\lambda}
\ \big|\ j_1,\dots,j_\ell\in I\,\big\}\ \smallsetminus\ \{0\}$.

The tensor product crystal is governed by Kashiwara's coproduct
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deltaminus}
\Delta_-(E_j)=E_j\ot K_j^{-1}+1\ot E_j,\qquad
\Delta_-(F_j)=F_j\ot1+K_j\ot F_j,\qquad
\Delta_-(K_j)=K_j\ot K_j.
\end{equation}
For two type-1 $\Uq$-modules $V,W$ denote by $V\ot_- W$ and $V\ot_+ W$ the tensor products formed via
\eqref{eq:deltaminus} and \eqref{eq:hopf}. For an element $b$ of a crystal set put
$\varepsilon_j(b)=\max\{n:\tilde e_j^{\,n}b\neq0\}$ and
$\varphi_j(b)=\max\{n:\tilde f_j^{\,n}b\neq0\}$, so that
$\varphi_j(b)-\varepsilon_j(b)=\langle\wt b,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle$. Then, for
$\lambda,\lambda'\in P_+$, the pair
$(L(\lambda)\ot L(\lambda'),\;B(\lambda)\ot B(\lambda'))$ is the lower crystal base of
$V(\lambda)\ot_-V(\lambda')$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensorrule}
\tilde e_j(b_1\ot b_2)=
\begin{cases}\tilde e_j b_1\ot b_2,&\varphi_j(b_1)\ge\varepsilon_j(b_2),\\
b_1\ot\tilde e_j b_2,&\varphi_j(b_1)<\varepsilon_j(b_2),\end{cases}
\qquad
\tilde f_j(b_1\ot b_2)=
\begin{cases}\tilde f_j b_1\ot b_2,&\varphi_j(b_1)>\varepsilon_j(b_2),\\
b_1\ot\tilde f_j b_2,&\varphi_j(b_1)\le\varepsilon_j(b_2).\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A node $b_1\ot b_2$ is a \emph{source} when $\tilde e_j$ annihilates it for all $j$,
and a \emph{sink} when $\tilde f_j$ does; by \eqref{eq:tensorrule} a source has $b_1$
the highest node and a sink has $b_2$ the lowest node.

\subsection{Quantum coordinate rings and generalized quantum minors}\label{ss:oq}

Let $G$ denote the simply connected simple complex algebraic group with Lie
algebra $\g$. For a finite dimensional type-1 $\Uq$-module $U$ and $u\in U$, $\xi\in U^*$, let
$c^{U}(\xi,u)\in\Uq^*$ denote the matrix coefficient $c^U(\xi,u)(y)=\langle\xi,y.u
\rangle$, and let $\mathcal{O}_q(G)\subseteq\Uq^*$ be the quantized coordinate ring spanned by matrix coefficients of all finite-dimensional type-1 $\Uq$-modules.

Let $\lambda\in P_+$ and $w_1,w_2\in W$. The extremal weight space $V(\lambda)_{w_2\lambda}$
is one-dimensional; fix a vector $v_{w_2\lambda}$ spanning it and let
$\xi_{w_1\lambda}\in(V(\lambda)^*)_{-w_1\lambda}$ be the dual functional of the vector $v_{w_1\lambda}\in V(\lambda)_{w_1\lambda}$ spanning $V(\lambda)_{w_1\lambda}$. The associated
\emph{generalized quantum minor} is
\[
\Delta_{w_1\lambda,\,w_2\lambda}:=c^{V(\lambda)}\big(\xi_{w_1\lambda},\,v_{w_2\lambda}\big)
\ \in\ \mathcal{O}_q(G),
\]
unique up to a non-zero scalar. For finite dimensional type-1 $\Uq$-modules $U_1$ and $U_2$
there is a $\Uq$-module isomorphism $U_2^*\ot U_1^*\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ (U_1\ot U_2)^*$ by assigning $\xi_2\otimes \xi_1\to [u_1\otimes u_2 \to \xi_1(u_1).\xi_2(u_2)]$
under which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult}
c^{U_1\ot U_2}(\xi_2\ot\xi_1,\ u_1\ot u_2)=c^{U_1}(\xi_1,u_1)\,c^{U_2}(\xi_2,u_2)
\end{equation}
for all $u_1\in U_1$, $u_2\in U_2$, $\xi_2\in U_2^*$ and $\xi_1\in U_1^*$.

\section{Quasi-minuscule modules}\label{sec:qm}
A non-zero dominant weight $\vp \in P_+$ is \emph{minuscule} if every weight of $V(\vp)$ lies in the Weyl group orbit of $\vp$. Similarly, a non-zero dominant weight $\vp \in P_+$ is called \emph{quasi-minuscule} if it is either minuscule or the only weight of $V(\vp)$ not in the orbit $W\cdot\vp$ is the zero weight. Each simple Lie algebra admits a unique quasi-minuscule highest weight module that is not minuscule with the highest weight = the highest short root of the associated root system. 
\subsection{The general construction}\label{ss:qmgen}
Let $\Phis\subseteq\Phi$ be the set
of short roots, with $\Phis=\Phi$ when $\g$ is simply-laced, and let
$\Pis:=\Pi\cap\Phis$ be the set of short simple roots. The set $\Phis$ forms a single
$W$-orbit containing a unique dominant weight $\vp$, the highest short root. The
quasi-minuscule module $V:=V(\vp)$ admits a weight basis
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qmbasis}
\{v_\mu\mid\mu\in\Phis\}\ \sqcup\ \{z_{\alpha_k}\mid\alpha_k\in\Pis\},
\qquad v_\mu\in V_\mu,\quad z_{\alpha_k}\in V_0,
\end{equation}
so that the non-zero weights of $V$ are all extremal weights, each of multiplicity one, and
$\dim V_0=|\Pis|$. Write $[2]_{q_j}=q_j+q_j^{-1}$. The
$\Uq$-module action on $V(\vp)$ is defined as follows \cite[5A.2]{Jantzen}.

\noindent\emph{(Q1)} For all $j\in I$,
\[
K_j\,v_\mu=q^{(\mu,\alpha_j)}v_\mu,\qquad K_j\,z_{\alpha_k}=z_{\alpha_k}.
\]

\noindent\emph{(Q2)} For $\mu\in\Phis$ with
$\mu\neq\pm\alpha_j$ one has $\langle\mu,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle\in\{0,\pm1\}$, and
\[
E_j\,v_\mu=
\begin{cases}v_{\mu+\alpha_j},&\langle\mu,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=-1,\\ 0,&\text{else},\end{cases}
\qquad
F_j\,v_\mu=
\begin{cases}v_{\mu-\alpha_j},&\langle\mu,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=1,\\ 0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}
\]

\noindent\emph{(Q3)} If $\alpha_j\notin\Pis$, then
$E_j\,z_{\alpha_k}=F_j\,z_{\alpha_k}=0$ for all $\alpha_k\in\Pis$.

\noindent\emph{(Q4)} If $\alpha_j\in\Pis$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_j\,v_{-\alpha_j}=z_{\alpha_j}, &\quad &E_j\,z_{\alpha_j}=[2]_{q_j}v_{\alpha_j}, &\quad &E_j\,v_{\alpha_j}=0,\\
&F_j\,v_{\alpha_j}=z_{\alpha_j}, &\quad &F_j\,z_{\alpha_j}=[2]_{q_j}v_{-\alpha_j}, &\quad &F_j\,v_{-\alpha_j}=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

\noindent\emph{(Q5)} If $\alpha_j,\alpha_k\in\Pis$ with
$j\neq k$, then
\[
E_j\,z_{\alpha_k}=
\begin{cases}v_{\alpha_j},&\langle\alpha_k,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=-1,\\ 0,&\text{else},\end{cases}
\qquad
F_j\,z_{\alpha_k}=
\begin{cases}v_{-\alpha_j},&\langle\alpha_k,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=-1,\\ 0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}
\]

When $\g$ is simply-laced, the weight $\vp$ is the highest root
and $V$ is the quantum adjoint representation; in this case the relations
\emph{(Q1)}--\emph{(Q5)} coincide verbatim with those of \cite[Theorem~5.10]{OQY} and
with Lusztig's description \cite{Lusztig} of the canonical basis of the quantum adjoint
representation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:crystal}
The $A_0$-lattice $L(\vp)\ =\ \bigoplus_{\mu\in\Phis}A_0\,v_\mu\ \oplus\ \bigoplus_{\alpha_k\in\Pis}A_0\,z_{\alpha_k}\ \subseteq\ V(\vp)$ is the lower crystal lattice of $V(\vp)$, with the highest weight vector $v_{\vp}$. Its reduction $B(\vp)\ =\ \{\,\overline{v}_\mu\mid\mu\in\Phis\,\}\ \sqcup\
\{\,\overline{z_{\alpha_k}}\mid\alpha_k\in\Pis\,\}\ \subseteq\ L(\vp)/qL(\vp)$
is the associated lower crystal basis.
\end{proposition}

We write $b_\mu:=\overline v_\mu\ (\mu\in\Phis),\;\;
b_{k,0}:=\overline{z_{\alpha_k}}\ (\alpha_k\in\Pis)$,
so that the highest node is $b_\vp$, the lowest node is $b_{-\vp}$ (because $w_0.\vp= -\vp$). For a short simple root $\alpha_k\in\Pis$, the node $b_{k,0}$ is the unique
zero-weight node of $B(\vp)$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zkstring}
\varepsilon_j(b_{k,0})=\varphi_j(b_{k,0})=\delta_{k,j}\qquad(j\in I).
\end{equation}
For a proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:crystal} and \eqref{eq:zkstring}, see Lemma 9.6.B of \cite{Jantzen} and the subsequent proof.

With this labeling, the quasi-minuscule weight is in fact a fundamental weight for these three types: $\vp=\vp_i$, where
$i=1\ \ (G_2),\;i=4\ \ (F_4),\;i=8\ \ (E_8)$, and we fix this index $i$ once and for all. As the diagrams show, in each of the three
types $\alpha_i$ is a \emph{short} simple root; thus $\alpha_i\in\Pis$, and
$z_{\alpha_i}\in V_0$ together with the zero-weight node
$b_{i,0}=\overline{z_{\alpha_i}}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:crystal} are well-defined.
Furthermore, $\dim V=7,\ 26,\ 248,\; m:=\dim V_0=|\Pis|=1,\ 2,\ 8$.

In each of these three types $-w_0=\mathrm{id}$ and $P=Q$; consequently
$(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb Z$ for all $\lambda,\mu\in P$. Moreover, $V$ appears as a direct summand in $V\ot V$ with multiplicity 1. We use short-hand notation $[V\ot V:V]=1$ to recall this fact later on (see Lemma \cite[5A.9b]{Jantzen}).

Applying \eqref{eq:tensorrule} to $B(\vp)\ot B(\vp)$ and using $[V\ot V:V]=1$, there is
a unique connected component $C\cong B(\vp)$ inside $B(\vp)\ot B(\vp)$; by the description of sources and sinks in
\S\ref{ss:crystal} its source and sink are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourcesink}
x=b_\vp\ot c\ (\wt c=0),\qquad x_{\mathrm{low}}=b'\ot b_{-\vp}\ (\wt b'=0),
\end{equation}
the weight conditions on $c$ and $b'$ being forced by $\wt x=\vp$ and
$\wt x_{\mathrm{low}}=-\vp$.

\section{Factorization of weights and the braiding twist}\label{sec:main}

\subsection{The two-sided weight control theorem}\label{ss:twosided}

The following proposition generalizes \cite[Lemma~3.1]{Dey}, which establishes that the
right tensor factor at each non-source node of $C$ has negative weight. We prove in
addition the dual statement for the left tensor factor, so that the weights of both
factors are controlled simultaneously for every node other than the source and sink
of $C$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:twosided}
Let $C\cong B(\vp)$ be the connected component of $B(\vp)\ot B(\vp)$ with source $x$
and sink $x_{\mathrm{low}}$ as in \eqref{eq:sourcesink}. Then $c=b'=b_{i,0}$, so that
$x=b_\vp\ot b_{i,0}$ and $x_{\mathrm{low}}=b_{i,0}\ot b_{-\vp}$. Moreover, every node
$y=b_1\ot b_2\in C$ with $y\neq x,\,x_{\mathrm{low}}$ satisfies $\wt b_1>0$ and $\wt b_2<0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\wt c=0$ and $c\neq b_\vp$, the node $c$ is not a source of $B(\vp)$, so
$\varepsilon_j(c)>0$ for some $j$. The source condition on $x$ forces
$\varepsilon_j(c)\le\varphi_j(b_\vp)=\langle\vp,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=\delta_{ji}$ for all
$j$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c}
\varepsilon_i(c)=1,\qquad \varepsilon_j(c)=0\quad(j\neq i).
\end{equation}
Since $\wt c=0$, this gives $\varphi_i(c)=\varepsilon_i(c)+\langle\wt c,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$
and $\varphi_j(c)=0$ for $j\neq i$; comparing with \eqref{eq:zkstring} identifies
$c=b_{i,0}$. Dually, $\wt b'=0$ and $b'\neq b_{-\vp}$ give
$\varphi_j(b')>0$ for some $j$, and the sink condition
$\varphi_j(b')\le\varepsilon_j(b_{-\vp})=\delta_{ji}$ forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b}
\varphi_i(b')=1,\qquad \varphi_j(b')=0\quad(j\neq i),
\end{equation}
so likewise $\varepsilon_i(b')=1$, $\varepsilon_j(b')=0$ $(j\neq i)$, and
\eqref{eq:zkstring} gives $b'=b_{i,0}=c$.

We first show that $\wt b_2<0$ for every $y\neq x$, arguing as in the proof of
\cite[Lemma~3.1]{Dey}. Since $C$ is connected with unique source $x$, any node
$y\neq x$ is of the form $y=\tilde f_{j_\ell}\cdots\tilde f_{j_1}x$ for some
$j_1,\dots,j_\ell$ with the intermediate applications nonzero. We induct on
$\ell$. For $\ell=1$ we compute $\tilde f_j x$. When $j\neq i$, \eqref{eq:c} gives
$\varphi_j(b_\vp)=0=\varepsilon_j(b_{i,0})$, so by \eqref{eq:tensorrule} the operator
$\tilde f_j$ acts on the right factor; but $\varphi_j(b_{i,0})=\varepsilon_j(b_{i,0})
+\langle\wt b_{i,0},\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=0$, so $\tilde f_j b_{i,0}=0$ and $\tilde f_j x=0$. When $j=i$,
$\varphi_i(b_\vp)=1=\varepsilon_i(b_{i,0})$, so $\tilde f_i$ again acts on the right, and
$\varphi_i(b_{i,0})=1$ yields
\[
\tilde f_i x=b_\vp\ot\tilde f_i b_{i,0},\qquad \wt(\tilde f_i b_{i,0})=\wt b_{i,0}-\alpha_i=-\alpha_i<0.
\]
Thus the assertion holds for $\ell=1$. For the inductive step, suppose $y=b_1\ot
b_2$ with $\wt b_2<0$, and let $y'=\tilde f_j y\neq0$. By \eqref{eq:tensorrule},
$\tilde f_j$ acts either on $b_1$, leaving $b_2$ unchanged, or on $b_2$, replacing
it by $\tilde f_j b_2$ of weight $\wt b_2-\alpha_j$. In either case the right
factor of $y'$ has negative weight, completing the induction.

We now show that $\wt b_1>0$ for every $y\neq x_{\mathrm{low}}$, by the dual
argument applied to the raising operators. As $C\cong B(\vp)$ has unique sink $x_{\mathrm{low}}$, any node $y\neq x_{\mathrm{low}}$ is of the form
$y=\tilde e_{j_\ell}\cdots\tilde e_{j_1}x_{\mathrm{low}}$ with the intermediate
applications nonzero, and we induct on $\ell$. For $\ell=1$ we compute $\tilde e_j
x_{\mathrm{low}}$. When $j\neq i$, \eqref{eq:b} gives
$\varphi_j(b_{i,0})=0=\varepsilon_j(b_{-\vp})$, so $\tilde e_j$ acts on the left factor;
but $\varepsilon_j(b_{i,0})=\varphi_j(b_{i,0})-\langle\wt b_{i,0},\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=0$, so $\tilde e_j
b_{i,0}=0$ and $\tilde e_j x_{\mathrm{low}}=0$. When $j=i$,
$\varphi_i(b_{i,0})=1=\varepsilon_i(b_{-\vp})$, so $\tilde e_i$ acts on the left, and
$\varepsilon_i(b_{i,0})=1$ yields
\[
\tilde e_i x_{\mathrm{low}}=(\tilde e_i b_{i,0})\ot b_{-\vp},\qquad
\wt(\tilde e_i b_{i,0})=\wt b_{i,0}+\alpha_i=\alpha_i>0.
\]
For the inductive step, suppose $y=b_1\ot b_2$ with $\wt b_1>0$ and let $y'=\tilde
e_j y\neq0$. By \eqref{eq:tensorrule}, $\tilde e_j$ acts either on $b_1$, replacing
it by $\tilde e_j b_1$ of weight $\wt b_1+\alpha_j$, or on $b_2$, leaving $b_1$
unchanged. In either case the left factor of $y'$ has positive weight.

A node $y$ distinct from both $x$ and $x_{\mathrm{low}}$ satisfies the hypotheses of
both inductions, so $\wt b_1>0$ and $\wt b_2<0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:Tminus}
There is a $\Uq$-morphism $T_-\colon V\ot_- V\to V$, carrying the product crystal lattice
onto $L(\vp)$, whose crystal limit $\overline{T_-}$ restricts to a bijection
$C\xrightarrow{\sim}B(\vp)$ and annihilates every other component. It has the
following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] For $\mu\in W\vp$ with $\mu\neq\pm\vp$, the unique node of $C$ of
weight $\mu$ is of the form $y=b_{\mu_1}\ot b_{\mu_2}$ with $\mu_1,\mu_2\in\Phis$,
$\mu_1>0>\mu_2$ and $\mu_1+\mu_2=\mu$; moreover
\[
T_-(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2})=c_\mu\,v_\mu,\qquad c_\mu\in A_0^\times.
\]
\item[\rm(ii)] The $m$ zero-weight nodes of $C$ are $b_{\nu_k}\ot b_{-\nu_k}$,
$k=1,2,\dots,m$, with distinct short roots $\nu_k>0$, and the vectors
$\{T_-(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})\}_{k=1}^m$ form a basis of $V_0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the uniqueness of crystal bases \cite[Theorem 3]{Kashiwara} there is a
lattice-preserving $\Uq$-module isomorphism $S\colon V\ot_- V\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigoplus_k
V(\lambda_k)$ whose crystal limit identifies $B(\vp)\ot B(\vp)$ with
$\bigsqcup_k B(\lambda_k)$. As $[V\ot V:V]=1$, one summand is $V(\vp)$ with crystal
$C\cong B(\vp)$; let $T_-=\pi_{V(\vp)}\circ S$. The stated properties of $\overline{T_-}$ are
those of the projection onto this summand.

For (i), write $y=b_1\ot b_2$ for the unique node of $C$ of weight $\mu$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:twosided}, $\wt b_1>0$ and $\wt b_2<0$; since the non-zero weights
of $B(\vp)$ are the short roots, each of multiplicity one, this forces
$b_1=b_{\mu_1}$ and $b_2=b_{\mu_2}$ for short roots $\mu_1>0>\mu_2$ with
$\mu_1+\mu_2=\wt y=\mu$. Now $\overline{T_-}(y)=b_\mu$ gives
$T_-(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2})\equiv v_\mu\pmod{qL(\vp)}$, and $\dim V_\mu=1$ lifts
the congruence to equality in (i) with $c_\mu\in A_0^\times$. For (ii), the zero-weight nodes are
neither the source nor the sink of $C$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:twosided} gives their
factor weights $\nu_k>0$ and $-\nu_k<0$; the images
$\overline{T_-}(b_{\nu_k}\ot b_{-\nu_k})$ are the $m$ distinct zero-weight
elements of $B(\vp)$, a basis of $L(\vp)_0/qL(\vp)_0$, and Nakayama's lemma ($A_0$
being a local ring) lifts them to an $A_0$-basis of $L(\vp)_0$ and hence a
$\K$-basis of $V_0$.
\end{proof}

Let $\psi\colon V\ot_- V\xrightarrow{\sim}V\ot_+ V$ be Kashiwara's isomorphism
\cite[(1.4.5)]{Kashiwara}, $\psi(u\ot v)=q^{(\wt u,\wt v)}u\ot v$ on
weight vectors. Then $T:=T_-\circ\psi^{-1}\colon V\ot_+ V\to V$ is a surjective
$\Uq$-morphism for \eqref{eq:hopf}, and $T(v\ot w)=q^{-(\wt v,\wt w)}T_-(v\ot w)$ on
weight vectors. Consequently Theorem~\ref{thm:Tminus} holds for $T$ over $\K$: with
$\mu,\mu_1,\mu_2$ as in {\rm(i)} and $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_m$ as in ${\rm(ii)}, T(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2})=q^{-(\mu_1,\mu_2)}c_\mu\,v_\mu, \;\;
q^{-(\mu_1,\mu_2)}c_\mu\in\K^\times,$
while $(\nu_k,-\nu_k)=-2$, so that the vectors
$T(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})=q^{2}\,T_-(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})$,
$k=1,2,\dots,m$, again form a basis of $V_0$.

\subsection{The braiding twist}\label{ss:braid}
For the course of the proof of the main result, we need to twist the morphism $T$ using the braid generator or braiding  $\Rhat$ of type-1 $\Uq$-modules. We shall quickly recall the construction of $\Rhat$ first. Let $\Theta=\sum_{\eta\in\Qp}\Theta_\eta$ be the quasi-$R$-matrix
\cite[Ch.~7]{Jantzen}, normalised by $\Theta_0=1\ot1$, with
$\Theta_\eta\in U^-_{-\eta}\ot U^+_{\eta}$. Write $f(v\ot w)=q^{-(\wt v,\wt
w)}v\ot w$ and $\tau(v\ot w)=w\ot v$, and set $\Rhat=\Theta\circ f\circ\tau\colon V\ot_+ V\to V\ot_+ V$,
a $\Uq$-isomorphism for \eqref{eq:hopf}. On weight vectors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rform}
\Rhat(v\ot w)=q^{-(\wt v,\wt w)}\,w\ot v
+\sum_{\eta>0}(u^-_{-\eta} w)\ot(u^+_\eta v), \;\;
\wt(u^-_{-\eta} w)<\wt w,\ \ \wt(u^+_\eta v)>\wt v.
\end{equation}
Because $\Rhat$ is an isomorphism and $[V\ot V:V]=1$, there is a scalar
$d\in\K^{\times}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TR}
T\circ\Rhat=d\,T.
\end{equation}
Recall from \S\ref{ss:threetypes} that $\alpha_i$, with $i=1,4,8$ in types $G_2$,
$F_4$, $E_8$ respectively, is the short simple root for which $\vp=\vp_i$; thus
$z_{\alpha_i}$ is a zero-weight vector of $V$ and $b_{i,0}=\overline{z_{\alpha_i}}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:d}
$d\neq1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $Z=v_\vp\ot z_{\alpha_i}\in V\ot_+ V$, a vector of weight $\vp$.
Suppose $d=1$; then $T(Z)=T(\Rhat Z)$ by \eqref{eq:TR}, and we evaluate both sides using $T=T_-\circ\psi^{-1}$.

As $\wt z_{\alpha_i}=0$, $\psi^{-1}(Z)=Z$, so $T(Z)=T_-(Z)$. The class of $Z$ in $C$ is the
source $x=b_\vp\ot b_{i,0}$, hence $T_-(Z)\equiv v_\vp\pmod{qL(\vp)}$; since
$\dim V_\vp=1$,
\[
T(Z)=\kappa\,v_\vp,\qquad \kappa\in1+qA_0,\qquad T(Z)\not\equiv0\pmod{qL(\vp)}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:Rform}, $\Rhat(Z)=q^{-(\vp,0)}z_{\alpha_i}\ot v_\vp+\sum_{\eta>0}(u^-_{-\eta}
z_{\alpha_i})\ot(u^+_\eta v_\vp)$. The scalar equals $1$, and each correction vanishes since
$u^+_\eta v_\vp=0$ for $\eta>0$, $v_\vp$ being highest. Thus $\Rhat(Z)=z_{\alpha_i}\ot
v_\vp$, and as $\wt z_{\alpha_i}=0$ again, $T(\Rhat Z)=T_-(z_{\alpha_i}\ot v_\vp)$.

The equality $T(Z)=T(\Rhat Z)$ becomes $T_-(v_\vp\ot z_{\alpha_i})=T_-(z_{\alpha_i}\ot v_\vp)$, so
$T_-(z_{\alpha_i}\ot v_\vp)\not\equiv0\pmod{qL(\vp)}$. But, the class of $z_{\alpha_i}\ot v_\vp$ in $C$ is $b_{i,0}\ot b_\vp$, we obtain
$\overline{T_-}(b_{i,0}\ot b_\vp)\neq0$. Therefore, $b_{i,0}\ot b_\vp$ must be in $C$. But
$C\cong B(\vp)$ and $\dim V_\vp=1$, so $C$ contains exactly one node of weight $\vp$, that is $x=b_\vp\ot b_{i,0}$, and $b_{i,0}\ot b_\vp\neq x$. This contradicts $b_{i,0}\ot b_\vp\in C$, so $d\neq1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:d}
In practice, $d$ can be computed explicitly. As $\Rhat^{\,2}$ is a $\Uq$-endomorphism
of $V\ot_+V$, it acts on each irreducible component by a scalar known as the monodromy eigenvalue. On the multiplicity free component of $V\ot_+ V$ isomorphic to $V$, this eigenvalue is exactly $d^2$, and it is of the form $d^{\,2}=q^{(\vp,\,\vp+2\rho)}$. Since $(\vp,\vp+2\rho)>0$, this gives $d^{\,2}\neq1$. For more details, we
refer to chapter~8 of \cite[Corollary 23]{KS} and \cite{Gould}.
\end{remark}

The following twisted version of $T$ will be used in the next section.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:extract}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] For $\mu\in W\vp$ with $\mu\neq\pm\vp$ and $\mu_1,\mu_2$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:Tminus}\,{\rm(i)},
\[
T\big(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2}-\Rhat(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2})\big)
=(1-d)\,T(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2})
=(1-d)\,q^{-(\mu_1,\mu_2)}c_\mu\,v_\mu
\]
is a nonzero scalar multiple of $v_\mu$.
\item[\rm(ii)] For $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_m$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:Tminus}\,{\rm(ii)},
the vectors
\[
T\big(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k}-\Rhat(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})\big)
=(1-d)\,T(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k}),\qquad k=1,2,\dots,m,
\]
form a basis of $V_0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\section{Generation via generalized quantum minors}\label{sec:minors}

Put $I_s:=\{k\in I\mid\alpha_k\in\Pis\}$, so that $|I_s|=m$, and write
$z_k:=z_{\alpha_k}$ for $k\in I_s$. Let
$\{\,v_\mu^*\mid\mu\in\Phis\,\}\sqcup\{\,z_k^*\mid k\in I_s\,\}$
be the basis of $V^*$ dual to the weight basis \eqref{eq:qmbasis} of $V$, so that
$v_\mu^*\in(V^*)_{-\mu}$ and $z_k^*\in(V^*)_0$. Set
\begin{gather*}
\mathscr{T}_{\neq0}:=\{\,c^V(v_\mu^*,v_{\mu'})\mid\mu,\mu'\in\Phis\,\},\\
\mathscr{T}_0:=\{\,c^V(z_k^*,v_\mu),\ c^V(v_\mu^*,z_k),\ c^V(z_k^*,z_l)
\mid\mu\in\Phis,\ k,l\in I_s\,\}.
\end{gather*}
Observe that $\mathscr{T}_{\neq0}=\{\,\Delta_{\mu,\mu'}\mid\mu,\mu'\in\Phis\,\}$
is exactly the set of generalized quantum minors of $V$. Let $\mathscr{T}''\subseteq \mathcal{O}_q(G)$ be the
$\K$-subalgebra generated by $\mathscr{T}_{\neq0}$. Fix $r\in I_s$. Since $z_r^*$ has weight $0$, equivariance of $T^*$ forces
$T^*(z_r^*)$ must be of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dagger}
T^*(z_r^*)=\underbrace{\sum_{k,l\in I_s} a^{(r)}_{kl}\,z_k^*\ot z_l^*}_{=:A_r}
\;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{\mu\in\Phis} n^{(r)}_{\mu}\,v_{\mu}^*\ot v_{-\mu}^*}_{=:N_r},
\qquad a^{(r)}_{kl},\,n^{(r)}_{\mu}\in\K .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Symmetry; {\rm cf.}\ {\rm\cite[Lemma~5.11]{OQY}}]\label{lem:sym}
The matrix $A^{(r)}=(a^{(r)}_{kl})_{k,l\in I_s}$ is symmetric for every $r\in I_s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $G_2$ we have $m=1$, so $A^{(r)}$ is $1\times1$ and the claim is trivial. For $\g=E_8$, it is simply-laced, so $\vp$ is the highest root and $V=V(\vp)$
is the quantum adjoint module. Therefore, \cite[Lemma~5.11]{OQY} gives the symmetry of $A^{(r)}$.

For $F_4$ we have $m=2$ and $\Pis=\{\alpha_3,\alpha_4\}$, so $I_s=\{3,4\}$. The two
short simple roots span a subsystem $\Phi(A_2)=\{\pm\alpha_3,\pm\alpha_4,
\pm\theta_{A_2}\}$ of type $A_2$, where $\theta_{A_2}:=\alpha_3+\alpha_4$, and
$E_j,F_j,K_j^{\pm1}$ $(j\in\{3,4\})$ generate a subalgebra
$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)\subseteq\Uq$. Write $V':=V_0\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Phi(A_2)}V_\mu$. It again follows that $V'$
is a $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)$-submodule of $V$, isomorphic to the adjoint module
$V_q(\theta_{A_2})$ and with zero-weight space $V'_0=V_0$. Now, $v_{\theta_{A_2}}$ and $v_{-\theta_{A_2}}$ are the highest and  lowest weight vectors
of $V'$, so $v_{\theta_{A_2}}\ot v_{-\theta_{A_2}}$ is a cyclic vector of the
$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)$-module $V'\ot V'$; since $T$ preserves weights, it sends this
vector into $V_0= V'_0\subseteq V'$, and hence
$T(V'\ot V')=U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)\,T(v_{\theta_{A_2}}\ot v_{-\theta_{A_2}})\subseteq V'$. Thus $T$ restricts to a $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)$-module homomorphism
$T|_{V'}\colon V'\ot V'\to V'$. By \eqref{eq:dagger} the matrix $A^{(r)}$ is the
$V_0^*\ot V_0^*$-component of $T^*(z_r^*)$, so it is the matrix attached to the pair
$\big(V'\cong V_q(\theta_{A_2}),\ T|_{V'}\big)$; as $A_2$ has rank $>1$,
\cite[Lemma~5.11]{OQY} again gives its symmetry.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[{\rm cf.}\ {\rm\cite[Theorem~5.9]{OQY}}]\label{lem:key}
Let $v,w\in V$ be weight vectors with $\wt v>0>\wt w$. Then 
$c^{V\ot V}\big(T^*(z_r^*),\ v\ot w-\Rhat(v\ot w)\big)\in\mathscr{T}'',
\;\; r\in I_s.$
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\zeta:=v\ot w-\Rhat(v\ot w)$ and split $T^*(z_r^*)=A_r+N_r$ as in
\eqref{eq:dagger}.

\emph{(a)} By \eqref{eq:Rform}, $\wt(u^-_{-\eta}w)<\wt w<0$ and
$\wt(u^+_\eta v)>\wt v>0$. Hence in $v\ot w$ and in every summand of
$\Rhat(v\ot w)$ both tensor slots carry non-zero weight.

\emph{(b)} Each summand of $N_r$ has both slots of non-zero weight, so by
\eqref{eq:mult} and (a) the pairing $c^{V\ot V}(N_r, \zeta)$ is a sum of products of
elements of $\mathscr{T}_{\neq0}$, hence lies in $\mathscr{T}''$.

\emph{(c)} For $y\in\Uq$, using $\Rhat\,\Delta(y)=\Delta(y)\Rhat$ and transporting
$\Rhat=\Theta\circ f\circ\tau$ to the dual side,
\[
\big\langle A_r,\ \Delta(y)\Rhat(v\ot w)\big\rangle
=\sum_{\eta\ge0}q^{(\eta,\eta)}\big\langle
\tau^*\big((S^{-1}\ot S^{-1})(\Theta_\eta^{21})A_r\big),\ \Delta(y)(v\ot w)\big\rangle .
\]
Since $\Theta_0=1\ot1$ and $\tau^*A_r=\sum_{k,l\in I_s}a^{(r)}_{lk}z_k^*\ot z_l^*=A_r$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}, the $\eta=0$ term equals
$\big\langle A_r,\Delta(y)(v\ot w)\big\rangle$ and cancels against the leading term.
Therefore
\[
c^{V\ot V}(A_r,\zeta)=-\sum_{\eta>0}q^{(\eta,\eta)}\,
c^{V\ot V}\big(\tau^*\big((S^{-1}\ot S^{-1})(\Theta_\eta^{21})A_r\big),\ v\ot w\big)
\ \in\ \mathscr{T}''.
\]
Therefore, we have $c^{V\ot V}\big(T^*(z_r^*),\ \zeta\big)\in\mathscr{T}''$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The main results}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\g$ be of type $G_2$, $F_4$ or $E_8$ and $V=V(\vp)$ the quasi-minuscule module.
Then $\mathscr{T}_0\subseteq\mathscr{T}''$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Step 1 ($W.\vp \setminus \{\pm \vp\}$). Let $\mu\in W\vp$ with $\mu\neq\pm\vp$, and let
$\mu_1,\mu_2$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:Tminus}\,(i). By
Corollary~\ref{cor:extract}(i),
$T\big(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2}-\Rhat(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2})\big)
=(1-d)\,q^{-(\mu_1,\mu_2)}c_\mu\,v_\mu$. The scalar $(1-d)q^{-(\mu_1,\mu_2)}c_\mu$ is non-zero, and $\mu_1>0>\mu_2$. Hence
\[
c^V(z_r^*,v_\mu)=\big((1-d)q^{-(\mu_1,\mu_2)}c_\mu\big)^{-1}
c^{V\ot V}\big(T^*(z_r^*),\ v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2}-\Rhat(v_{\mu_1}\ot v_{\mu_2})\big)
\in\mathscr{T}''
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:key}, for every $r\in I_s$ and every short root $\mu\neq\pm\vp$.

Step 2 ($\vp\;\text{and} -\vp$). We show $c^V(z_r^*,v_{\pm\vp})\in\mathscr{T}''$ for
all $r\in I_s$. By construction of $T$, the source $x=b_\vp\ot b_{i,0}$ and sink
$x_{\mathrm{low}}=b_{i,0}\ot b_{-\vp}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:twosided} give the relations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step2rel}
T(v_\vp\ot z_i)=\kappa_+\,v_\vp,\qquad
T(z_i\ot v_{-\vp})=\kappa_-\,v_{-\vp},\qquad \kappa_\pm\in\K^\times,
\end{equation}
where $i\in I_s$ is the index fixed in \S\ref{ss:threetypes}. Applying $F_iE_i$ to the first relation and using \emph{(Q1)}--\emph{(Q5)}, a direct computation shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step2key}
T(v_{\vp-\alpha_i}\ot v_{\alpha_i})=\kappa'\,v_\vp,\qquad \kappa'\in\K^\times.
\end{equation}
Here both tensor slots carry positive weight, $\vp-\alpha_i>0$ and $\alpha_i>0$, so
pairing \eqref{eq:step2key} with $z_r^*$ through \eqref{eq:mult} and splitting
$T^*(z_r^*)=A_r+N_r$ gives
\[
\kappa'\,c^V(z_r^*,v_\vp)=c^{V\ot V}\big(N_r,\,v_{\vp-\alpha_i}\ot v_{\alpha_i}\big)
+\sum_{k,l\in I_s}a^{(r)}_{kl}\,c^V(z_l^*,v_{\vp-\alpha_i})\,c^V(z_k^*,v_{\alpha_i}).
\]
The first term is a sum of products of elements of $\mathscr{T}_{\neq0}$; in the
second, each factor has a zero-weight functional and extremal vector with weight
$\vp-\alpha_i,\alpha_i\in W\vp\setminus\{\pm\vp\}$, hence lies in $\mathscr{T}''$ by
Step~1. Thus $c^V(z_r^*,v_\vp)\in\mathscr{T}''$. Applying $E_iF_i$ to the
second relation of \eqref{eq:step2rel} gives
$T(v_{-\alpha_i}\ot v_{-\vp+\alpha_i})=\kappa''\,v_{-\vp}$ with $\kappa''\in\K^\times$
and both slots of negative weight, and hence $c^V(z_r^*,v_{-\vp})\in\mathscr{T}''$ by a similar argument. Together with Step~1, this gives
$c^V(z_r^*,v_\mu)\in\mathscr{T}''$ for all $\mu\in\Phis$ and all $r\in I_s$.

Step 3 (zero-weight vectors). Let $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_m$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:Tminus}\,(ii). By Corollary~\ref{cor:extract}(ii) the vectors
$T\big(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k}-\Rhat(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})\big)$,
$k=1,2,\dots,m$, form a basis of $V_0$, and $\nu_k>0>-\nu_k$. Writing
$z_j=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda^{(j)}_k\,
T\big(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k}-\Rhat(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})\big)$ with
$\lambda^{(j)}_k\in\K$, we get 
$c^V(z_r^*,z_j)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda^{(j)}_k\,
c^{V\ot V}\big(T^*(z_r^*),\ v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k}
-\Rhat(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})\big)\in\mathscr{T}''$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:key}, for all $r,j\in I_s$.

Step 4 (transposed family). Let $\mu\in\Phis$ and $j\in I_s$. With
$z_j=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda^{(j)}_k(1-d)\,T(v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k})$ as in Step~3, $c^V(v_\mu^*,z_j)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda^{(j)}_k(1-d)\,
c^{V\ot V}\big(T^*(v_\mu^*),\,v_{\nu_k}\ot v_{-\nu_k}\big).$ Therefore, using Step~1, and Step~2 we immediately conclude that $c^V(v_\mu^*,z_j)\in\mathscr{T}''$.

Steps 1--4 exhaust $\mathscr{T}_0$ and this settles the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:allG}
Let $G$ be a simply connected simple complex algebraic group. Then $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$
is generated as a $\K$-algebra by generalized quantum minors.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $\g$ be the Lie algebra of $G$. If $\g$ is of type $A_n$, $B_n$, $C_n$, $D_n$,
$E_6$ or $E_7$, then $\g$ admits a family of non-trivial
minuscule modules that are also a tensor generating family, so $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ is generated
by their matrix coefficients. Every weight of a minuscule module is extremal, so each of these matrix coefficients is a generalized quantum minor. Hence, the assertion follows.

In the remaining types $G_2$, $F_4$, and $E_8$, there are no non-trivial minuscule modules. Here
the quasi-minuscule module $V=V(\vp)$ is a tensor generator, so $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ is
generated by the matrix coefficients of $V$, that is by
$\mathscr{T}_{\neq0}\sqcup\mathscr{T}_0$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:main} we have
$\mathscr{T}_0\subseteq\mathscr{T}''$. So, $\mathcal{O}_q(G)=\mathscr{T}''$ is
generated by $\mathscr{T}_{\neq0}$, which is the set of generalized
quantum minors of $V(\vp)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{srings}
Corollary~\ref{cor:allG} is stated over $\K=\mathbb Q(q)$. In fact \cite{OQY}
establish a sharp refinement of it over much smaller ground rings: for types $A_n$,
$B_n$, $C_n$, $D_n$, $E_6$ and $E_7$ the corresponding statement holds over the
Laurent ring $\mathcal A=\mathbb Z[q^{\pm1/2}]$, and for $G_2$ over
$\mathcal A\big[(q^2+1)^{-1}\big]$. The $E_8$ case, however, is still obtained only
over $\mathbb Q(q^{1/2})$, and nothing concerning $F_4$ is mentioned there.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cluster}
Let $G$ be of type $F_4$. Then, $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ admits a quantized cluster
algebra structure over $\mathbb Q(q^{1/2})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:allG} gives in type $F_4$ the generation of $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ via generalized quantum minors. It immediately implies that $\mathcal{O}_q(G)$ admits a quantized cluster algebra structure over $\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/2})$ by the same argument given before
\cite[Theorem~5.4]{OQY}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
It is worthwhile to mention that Corollary \ref{cor:allG} for $G=F_4$ can not be specialized at $q=1$. To be precise, the quasi-minuscule module $V(\vp_4)$ appears in the symmetric subspace of the tensor product module $V(\vp_4)\otimes V(\vp_4)$, we have $d=1$ ($\hat{R}$ is replaced by the flip)  for $\mathcal{O}(F_4)$ (see Remark \ref{rem:d}). Therefore, the factor $\frac{1}{1-d}$ repeatedly used in the proof of the Theorem \ref{thm:main} blows up as $q\to 1$. In \cite[Remark 3.10]{Oya}, Oya explained why his techniques also could not touch the $F_4$ case in the classical setting. At this point, the most promising approach to settle this problem looks like to reprove Corollary \ref{cor:allG} for $F_4$ over the ring of Laurent polynomials $\mathcal{A}$ and then specialize at $q=1$. But we do not have proof of this yet. 
\end{remark}
