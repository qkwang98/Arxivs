\section{The odd moment differential algebra}
\label{app:odd-derived}

The two-column reduction has a simple differential-algebra model.
It makes the ring duality explicit and identifies the minimal
syzygy-generating layer of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-column-module}
as values of triple Massey products. Throughout, $m=n-1$ and
$\mathcal E$, $\omega$, $L$, $c$, and $P_e$ have the meanings of
Section~\ref{sec:two-column-homology}.

\subsection{Totalization and the Heisenberg algebra}

On a purely odd two-dimensional row space, the only moment generators
are
$u=\sum_i\theta_i$, $v=\sum_i\phi_i$, and
$q=\sum_i\theta_i\phi_i$.
Let $\xi,\zeta,\eta$ be their Koszul generators. For internal degree
$D$ and homological degree $j$, assign cohomological degree $D-j$
and twist the product by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-totalization}
                       a\star b=(-1)^{D(a)j(b)}ab.
\end{equation}
The original commutation sign
$(-1)^{D(a)D(b)+j(a)j(b)}$ becomes the cohomological commutation
sign, and the differential satisfies the corresponding Leibniz rule
without modification.

Now $\xi,\zeta$ have cohomological degree zero, with
$d\xi=u$ and $d\zeta=v$. Centering the odd variables and making the
$GL(U)$-equivariant change
\[
 \omega=q-\frac{uv}{n},\qquad
 \eta'=\eta-\frac{\xi\star v-\zeta\star u}{2n}
\]
gives $d\eta'=\omega$. The two factors
$(K[\xi]\otimes\Lambda(u),d\xi=u)$ and
$(K[\zeta]\otimes\Lambda(v),d\zeta=v)$ have only constant cohomology
in characteristic zero. Their joint augmentation is a
quasi-isomorphism onto
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-heisenberg-model}
 A_m=\bigl(\Lambda(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m,
                  \phi_1,\ldots,\phi_m,\eta),\,d\eta=\omega\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is the Chevalley--Eilenberg algebra of the $(2m+1)$-dimensional
Heisenberg Lie algebra, up to the sign convention for its central
generator. Its cohomology is classical; see \cite{CairnsJambor}.
Here it is the actual complete odd specialization of the moment
complex, with its product.

Put $R=\mathcal E/(\omega)$ and
$I=\eta\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal E}(\omega)$. Cycles and
boundaries in \eqref{eq:odd-heisenberg-model} give the ring identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-cohomology-double}
 H^*(A_m)=R\oplus I,\qquad I^2=0.
\end{equation}
The exterior top-product pairing identifies $I^j$ with the dual of
$R^{2m+1-j}$, tensored with the orientation line
$(\det U)^{m+1}\otimes(\det W)^2$.
The orientation class is $\eta\omega^m/m!$, of cohomological degree
$2n-1$ and internal degree $2n$. Thus \eqref{eq:odd-cohomology-double}
is a Poincar\'e-duality trivial extension. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-column-module} and this pairing,
$\operatorname{Soc}(R)=R_m=P_m$; its type is the Catalan number
$\frac1{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}$, so $R$ is not Gorenstein when $n\ge2$.
Also,
\[
 \dim R_e=\binom{2m}{e}-\binom{2m}{e-2}\quad(0\le e\le m),
 \qquad
 \dim H^*(A_m)=\binom{2n}{n}.
\]

Fixing $\omega$ gives an action of $Sp_{2m}$ on the differential
algebra. Here $P_0=K$ and $P_e$ is the primitive fundamental
representation for $1\le e\le m$. This larger symmetry mixes row
components; a fixed $GL(U)$ highest-weight space need not be stable.

\subsection{Massey products and all positive homology}

For $\beta\in U\otimes W$, set
$\iota_\beta=[c,\varepsilon_\beta]$ and
$T_\beta=\varepsilon_\beta\iota_\beta$.
In Darboux coordinates,
$\iota_{\theta_i}=\partial_{\phi_i}$ and
$\iota_{\phi_i}=-\partial_{\theta_i}$.
The operator $T_\beta$ is the rank-one symplectic infinitesimal
transformation, acting as a derivation on $\mathcal E$.

\begin{theorem}[Massey generation of positive moment homology]
\label{thm:odd-massey-generation}
For $m\ge1$, $\alpha\in P_m$, and $\beta\in U\otimes W$, the triple
Massey product is defined, has zero indeterminacy, and equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-massey-action}
 \langle[\alpha],[\beta],[\beta]\rangle
       =\{(-1)^m[\eta T_\beta\alpha]\}.
\end{equation}
Its values span the minimal generator space $\eta P_m$ and generate
all positive moment homology as an $R$-module.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $L\alpha=0$, the identity
$[L,\iota_\beta]=\varepsilon_\beta$ gives
$L\iota_\beta\alpha=\beta\alpha$.
Use the convention that $dx=ab$, $dy=bc$ represents the triple
product by $ay+(-1)^{|a|+1}xc$.
Taking $x=(-1)^m\eta\iota_\beta\alpha$ and $y=0$ proves
\eqref{eq:odd-massey-action}. Its indeterminacy is
$[\alpha]H^1(A_m)+H^m(A_m)[\beta]=0$:
these groups have no $\eta$ part, and their indicated products lie
in $R^{m+1}=0$.

Polarizing $T_\beta$ identifies their span with
$\mathfrak{sp}_{2m}\cong\Sym^2(U\otimes W)$.
The middle primitive module $P_m$ is nontrivial and irreducible, so
$\mathfrak{sp}_{2m}P_m=P_m$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-column-module} now gives the final assertion.
\end{proof}

For example,
\[
 \left\langle[\theta_1\cdots\theta_m],[\phi_1],[\phi_1]\right\rangle
       =\{[\eta\theta_2\cdots\theta_m\phi_1]\}
\]
is nonzero. Consequently the odd moment differential algebra is
nonformal for every $n\ge2$.
For $n=1$ the reduced algebra is $\Lambda(\eta)$ and is formal.
These assertions concern this odd specialization, not every
bosonic specialization.

\subsection{The complete ternary operation}

Define the Lefschetz partial inverse $J$ by
\[
 J(L^jf)=L^{j-1}f\quad(j>0),\qquad J(f)=0\quad(f\in P_e),
 \qquad \pi=1-LJ,\quad Q=1-JL.
\]
The operators $\pi$ and $Q$ project to initial and terminal string
vectors, respectively. Identify $R$ with $\pi(\mathcal E)$.

\begin{proposition}[A minimal model with only binary and ternary operations]
\label{prop:odd-ternary-model}
There is a minimal $A_\infty$ model on $R\oplus I$, with its usual
cohomology product, for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-ternary-formula}
 m_3(a,b,c)=\eta Q\bigl(J(ab)c-aJ(bc)\bigr)
                                   \qquad(a,b,c\in R).
\end{equation}
It has $m_3=0$ if an input lies in $I$, and $m_r=0$ for every $r\ge4$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
There is an internal-degree-preserving deformation retract with
$h(a)=\eta J(a)$, $h(\eta b)=0$ and
$p(a+\eta b)=\pi(a)+\eta Q(b)$.
If $i$ includes the chosen representatives, then $dh+hd=1-ip$.
Apply homotopy transfer, with the cohomological conventions of
\cite[Section~3]{KellerAInfinity}:
\[
 \sum_{r+s+t=N}(-1)^{r+st}
 m_{r+1+t}
   (\operatorname{id}^{\otimes r}\otimes m_s
                  \otimes\operatorname{id}^{\otimes t})=0.
\]
The tensor products of maps carry their Koszul signs.
The two ternary transfer terms are
$p(h(ab)c-(-1)^{|a|}a h(bc))$; moving $a$ across $\eta$ gives
\eqref{eq:odd-ternary-formula}.
Every transferred operation preserves internal degree. Since
$|m_r|=2-r$, its output moment-homological index is
$\sum j_{\mathrm{in}}+r-2$. Only indices zero and one survive,
which proves the vanishing assertions.
\end{proof}

In this convention
$m_3(\alpha,\beta,\beta)=-\eta T_\beta\alpha$ for $\alpha\in P_m$.
The Massey convention in \eqref{eq:odd-massey-action} differs by
$(-1)^{m+1}$, as it must. Formula~\eqref{eq:odd-ternary-formula}
also specifies the operation when an ordinary Massey product is
not defined. The cutoff concerns this chosen bigraded minimal
model, not all possible higher ordinary Massey products.
