\subsection{Finite jets and their maps}

The stable multiplicities depend on the graded augmentation space.
The compatible parabolic algebras retain more: they recover
multiplication, and also every map of the finite jets.  Here the
algebras need not be graded.  Put $J_n(B)=B/\mathfrak m_B^n$.

\begin{theorem}[Full faithfulness of finite jets]
\label{thm:finite-jet-full-faithfulness}
Let $n\ge2$, let $K$ be a field in which $n!$ is invertible, and
let $B,C$ be augmented commutative $K$-algebras.  There is a natural
bijection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-jet-full-faithfulness}
 \Hom_{S_n\text{-alg}}(Q_n(B),Q_n(C))
 \cong
 \Hom_{\mathrm{aug}\text{-alg}}(J_n(B),J_n(C)).
\end{equation}
Maps on the left are unital; they automatically preserve augmentation.
Thus $Q_n$ is fully faithful on augmented algebras whose augmentation
ideals have $n$th power zero.  If the two jets are finite dimensional,
\eqref{eq:finite-jet-full-faithfulness} is an isomorphism of affine
schemes of homomorphisms, including the loci of isomorphisms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-first-parabolic-jet}, the first factor
map $B\to Q_n(B)$ kills $\mathfrak m_B^n$.  The same is true in
every factor, by permutation of labels.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-jet-truncation}
                         Q_n(B)\cong Q_n(J_n(B)).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the factor maps give the inverse to the evident quotient
map: the invariant ideal is generated by global traces of
augmentation elements, and these traces vanish under the factor
maps.

Reynolds averaging and the definition give $Q_n(B)^{S_n}=K$.
Its Reynolds projection therefore equals its augmentation, since
augmentation commutes with averaging and is the identity on $K$.
An equivariant unital algebra map commutes with this projection
and hence preserves augmentation.  Restrict it to the invariants
of $S_{n-1}$ fixing the first factor.  The first-parabolic theorem
gives an augmented map $J_n(B)\to J_n(C)$.  Conversely, apply
$Q_n$ to any such map and use \eqref{eq:finite-jet-truncation}.
These operations are inverse: $Q_n(B)$ is generated by the
$S_n$-orbit of its first factor.  They are natural in both algebras.

For finite-dimensional jets, repeat this argument after every
commutative $K$-algebra extension $R$.  Tensor powers, the
invariant-generated ideal, and Reynolds idempotents commute with
base change; the first parabolic is $J_n(B)\otimes_K R$.
The same inverse constructions therefore work over nonreduced
$R$ as well.  Yoneda's lemma identifies the affine homomorphism
schemes.  Applying the construction to a map and its inverse
identifies the isomorphism loci.
\end{proof}

Applying augmentation to the last tensor factor sends invariant traces
on $n$ factors to those on $n-1$ factors, defining $Q_n(B)\to Q_{n-1}(B)$.
On first parabolics this is the quotient map
$B/\mathfrak m_B^n\to B/\mathfrak m_B^{n-1}$.

\begin{corollary}[Formal towers]
\label{cor:formal-towers}
In characteristic zero, compatible equivariant algebra maps between
the towers $\{Q_n(B)\}_{n\ge2}$ and $\{Q_n(C)\}_{n\ge2}$ are
exactly the continuous augmented algebra maps
$\widehat B_{\mathfrak m_B}\to\widehat C_{\mathfrak m_C}$.
The transition maps augment the last tensor factor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The theorem identifies compatible tower maps with compatible maps
of the jet quotients.  Taking inverse limits gives a continuous augmented
map of completions.  Conversely, such a map takes the closure of
$\mathfrak m_B^n$ into the closure of $\mathfrak m_C^n$: approximate
elements of the former by sums of products of $n$ augmentation
elements and use continuity.  It therefore induces the required
maps at every finite level.
\end{proof}

For example, take
\[
 B_1=K[x]/(x^3),\quad \deg x=1,
 \qquad
 B_2=K[u,v]/(u,v)^2,\quad \deg u=1,\quad\deg v=2.
\]
Their graded augmentation spaces are isomorphic: each has one
basis vector in degrees one and two.  After choosing this
identification, Theorem~\ref{thm:stable-augmented-multiplicities}
gives the same stable multiplicity spaces.  Their first parabolics
at $n=2$ differ, since $\dim J_2(B_1)=2$ and
$\dim J_2(B_2)=3$.  Thus the stable representation and the
parabolic algebra record different information.

\begin{corollary}[Equivariant automorphisms of polynomial coinvariants]
\label{cor:polynomial-jet-automorphisms}
Let $n\ge2$, let $K$ have characteristic zero, and let $\dim V=k$.
For $R_{n,k}=Q_n(\Sym V)$, the equivariant
automorphism group scheme is the group of origin-fixing coordinate
substitutions modulo degree $n$ with invertible linear part.  It is
smooth of dimension
\[
                 k\left(\binom{k+n-1}{k}-1\right),
\]
and its graded subgroup is $\GL(V)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
An augmented endomorphism of $\Sym V/(V)^n$ is specified freely
by the image of $V$ in $(V)/(V)^n$.  It is invertible exactly
when its linear part is invertible, as an inverse can then be
constructed successively by degree.  Its scheme is the product,
as a variety, of $\GL(V)$ and the vector space of higher
coefficients.  Count these coefficients and apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-jet-full-faithfulness}.
\end{proof}
