\fronttitle{Extended Abstract}
\begin{spacing}{1.5}

\nid The notion of defects in quantum field theories (QFTs) has amassed an extraordinary amount of attention and importance in physics and, perhaps surprisingly, also in mathematics. Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-dimensional manifold on which a given QFT $\tau$ lives. A defect of dimension $d$ can be heuristically articulated as the data of a submanifold $D\subset M$ of codimension $(n-d)$, together with some discontinuity of $\tau$ along $D$ which produces another quantum field theory. Examples of these phenomena are given by Wilson and ’t Hooft line defects in Yang--Mills theory. Giving a definition and a classification of these general defects is the subject of extensive ongoing work (see \cite{CEG23,CZR25}).

QFTs which lack dependence on any geometric parameter are dubbed \emph{topological QFTs} (TQFTs). In particular, defects whose interaction with the bulk theory is independent of the isotopy class of their embedding are called topological defects. One can organize topological QFTs with topological defects into a single package, called a defect (or stratified) topological quantum field theory. These are symmetric monoidal functors out of the category of (oriented) stratified cobordisms with defect data into the category of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces
\[\cZ \maps \mathsf{Bord}_{d}^{\sf strat} \longra \mathsf{Vect}_{\CC}^{\sf f.d}.\]
Extending these categories upwards — by adding stratified diffeomorphisms and stratified isotopies between them, and higher stratified isotopies between these, etc. — we can contemplate derived versions of these defect TQFTs; whereas by extending them downwards — letting stratified bordisms be cut into stratified cornered pieces of arbitrary depth — we can contemplate more computable versions of these derived defect TQFTs. This thesis is concerned with building such an extended object.

In his sketch of a proof of the \emph{Cobordism Hypothesis} \cite{Lur09}, Lurie introduces the first incarnation of the fully extended $(\oo,d)$-category of framed cobordisms as a (complete) $d$-fold Segal space. In the last section of that paper, he gives a very rough \emph{sketch} of a definition for an $(\oo,d)$-category of cobordisms with singularities through a convoluted notion of $\vec{X}$-manifold with singularities. In principle, this is the object we are after; but in practice, this class of spaces lacks differential-topological foundations that would enable its widespread use. We revise his construction in three ways: 1) we use a different model for higher categories; 2) we make use of the theory of conically smooth stratified spaces and their tangential structures; 3) we limit our construction to stratified bordisms whose underlying space is a topological manifold.

We now provide a general outline of the thesis. In Chapter 2 we introduce a model for $(\oo,1)$-categories that complements the existing literature, which we call \emph{interval Segal spaces} — these are simplicial co-presheaves on $\Int$, a subcategory of the poset of opens of $[0,1]$. In particular, we construct a model structure on $\mathsf{coPsh}(\Int)$ which is Quillen equivalent to that of Rezk’s Segal spaces. The motivation here is geometric: most models of $(\oo,1)$-categories are not tailored for the construction of geometric moduli spaces. A rare exception is the recent work of Ayala--Francis--Rozenblyum \cite{AFR1}, where the authors present \emph{striation sheaves} — a highly bespoke class of constructible sheaves on stratified spaces, designed precisely for constructing $(\oo,1)$-categories of a moduli flavour using point-set data. Our construction attempts a similar feat, while being less ambitious and remaining closer to more traditional Segal spaces, thereby facilitating higher-dimensional versions thereof.

In Chapter 3 we present a new construction of the $d$-dimensional bordism $(\oo,1)$-category. This construction is a spiritual cousin of other works, namely those of Galatius--Madsen--Tillman--Weiss \cite{GMTW09}, which later inspired the work of Lurie \cite{Lur09}, and more recently that of Scheimbauer--Calaque \cite{CS19}.

The pioneering work of Galatius--Madsen--Tillman--Weiss offered the first tools to construct an $(\oo,1)$-category of cobordisms. In their paper on the homotopy type of the cobordism category, they construct a topologically enriched category of $d$-dimensional bordisms using classifying spaces. Building on their work, Lurie, in his sketch of a proof of the cobordism hypothesis \cite{Lur09}, put forth a first construction of a model for an $(\oo,n)$-category of cobordisms using $n$-fold complete Segal spaces. This construction contained a small mistake preventing it from satisfying the \emph{essential constancy} condition. In order to address this, in her PhD thesis and in a subsequent paper, Scheimbauer--Calaque propose an alternative definition, which fixes Lurie’s while retaining the use of $n$-fold complete Segal spaces. Despite this almost successful attempt at a \emph{corrigendum}, the work of Scheimbauer--Calaque contains a separate flaw in its claim that the simplicial sets modelling the spatial levels of its $n$-fold Segal spaces are Kan complexes — a claim which, being false, translates into a loss of fibrancy necessary for the proof of the Segal condition.

To remedy this, we make use of the theory of smooth $\oo$-sheaves, i.e. $\oo$-sheaves on the site of smooth manifolds, and their respective classifying spaces — as articulated in the work of Berwick-Evans--Boavida de Brito--Pavlov \cite{BBEP24} — as a conduit for constructing appropriate moduli spaces of smooth manifolds, smooth bordisms, and smooth families of composed bordisms. This gives our approach a distinct flavour and clarifies the minor pathologies identified in previous constructions.

Let us sketch the path we will take in this section. We start by defining a general construction $\widetilde{\bord}_{d,V}(-,-)$ which is a bifunctor on $\man^{\op}\times \Int$ that, when fixing $T\in \Int$, produces a sheaf of sets on $\man^{\op}$ and captures a type of moduli space construction for $d$-dimensional smooth manifolds embedded in $V$ and fibred over smooth manifolds of varying dimension — designed so that, upon taking its concordance resolution and further restricting along the cosimplicial manifold $\bDelta \ra \man$, we obtain a simplicial set $\bord_{d,V}$, or space, which we show is the classifying space of $(d-1)$-dimensional manifolds, $d$-dimensional bordisms, and composites of $d$-dimensional bordisms. Lastly, the most dense and technical part of this chapter is spent showing that this construction, a pre-interval space, is an interval Segal space which recovers the $d$-dimensional bordism category $\mathsf{Cob}_d$ as its homotopy 1-category.

In Chapter 4 we finally turn to the theory of \emph{conically smooth stratified spaces}. These were first introduced in the work of Ayala--Francis--Tanaka and further elaborated in the subsequent works of Ayala--Francis--Rozenblyum \cite{AFT1, AFR1, AFR2}. They form the geometric foundations for our stratified higher bordism categories, and their adoption for that role is undoubtedly one of the more novel features of this thesis.

This chapter contains a heavier dose of exposition. We introduce the necessary differential-topological toolkit for our construction of the stratified bordism category, namely: we introduce an $(\oo,1)$-category of conically smooth stratified spaces; the exit-path $(\oo,1)$-category; and the constructible tangent bundle — all of which are necessary for a discussion of stratified tangential structures thereon. We prove new theorems about the contractibility of spaces of embeddings of stratified spaces into Euclidean space, and we prove a stratified analogue of the famed Ehresmann fibration theorem — which is the key to unlocking comparisons with the infrastructure developed for the unstratified case.

In Chapter 5 we construct an interval Segal space, i.e. an $(\oo,1)$-category of conically smooth stratified $d$-dimensional bordisms. We begin by introducing the notion of a conically smooth stratified topological $d$-manifold by stipulating a suitable $(\oo,1)$-category of basics $\Bsc_{d}^{\cS_{d-1}}$, whose links are stratified spheres of appropriate codimensions. Secondly, we produce a pre-interval space $\cS \bord_d$ of stratified topological $d$-dimensional bordisms, again using the concordance resolution for smooth $\oo$-sheaves and a restriction along the cosimplicial smooth manifold $\bDelta \longra \man$, and show that it is an interval Segal space.

Lastly, we endow this interval Segal space with a symmetric monoidal structure agreeing with the expected one coming from disjoint union. After defining this symmetric monoidal interval Segal space, we initiate a discussion on suitable notions of tangential structures for stratified bordisms — in particular, we introduce the notion of \emph{oriented discrete defect datum} by way of discrete right fibrations over $\Bsc_{d}^{\cS_{d-1}}$, as an attempt to make contact with the defect data for stratified bordisms introduced in the work of Carqueville et al. (see \cite{CRS19}) and we prove a comparison result for the stratified oriented case $d=2$.

We conclude Chapter~5 by endowing the resulting interval Segal space of conically smooth stratified $d$-bordisms with the symmetric monoidal structure induced by disjoint union. We then initiate a discussion of tangential structures in the stratified setting and, in particular, explain how discrete defect data can be encoded as tangential data via discrete right fibrations over the chosen $(\oo,1)$-category of basics. As a first test of this perspective, we prove a comparison result in the oriented stratified case $d=2$. Finally, we define a (derived) \emph{stratified topological quantum field theory} to be a symmetric monoidal functor from the symmetric monoidal $(\oo,1)$-category of stratified bordisms constructed here to a symmetric monoidal $(\oo,1)$-category $\cC$. 

Chapter 6 is a future outlook. In the first section, following ideas in unpublished work of Manuel Ara\'ujo and Pedro Boavida de Brito, we introduce a higher-dimensional generalization of $\Int$, denoted $\cube_d$ for $d>1$, aimed at extending interval Segal techniques to the study of $(\oo,d)$-categories. We prove that simplicial co-presheaves on $\cube_d$ satisfying suitable higher-dimensional analogues of the Segal and completeness conditions present the $(\oo,1)$-category $\Cat_{(\oo,d)}$, and we outline how this formalism should be used to build a $\cube_d$-space of stratified bordisms and verify the corresponding Segal condition, thus providing a candidate model for fully extended stratified TQFTs \`a la Atiyah--Baez--Dolan--Lurie. In the second section we return to the non-extended setting and sketch how constructible factorization algebras and \emph{stratified factorization homology} can be leveraged as a mechanism for producing stratified (and derived) defect TQFTs from appropriate stratified disk-algebras, in the spirit of the factorization-homological approach of Scheimbauer and Calaque.

\begin{lemma}
    There is a functor $\mathsf{disc}\maps \set \longrightarrow \Cat$ which sends a set $X$ to the discrete category on $X$. This category admits a left adjoint 
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
            \Cat \&\& \set
            \arrow[""{name=0, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "{\pi_0}", curve={height=-12pt}, from=1-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[""{name=1, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "{\mathsf{disc}}", curve={height=-12pt}, from=1-3, to=1-1]
            \arrow["\dashv"{anchor=center, rotate=-90}, draw=none, from=0, to=1]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    which sends a category to its set of \textbf{path-components}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The functor $\mathsf{disc}$ sends products of sets $X\times Y$ to products of categories $\mathsf{disc}(X)\times \mathsf{disc}(Y)$ and it sends the terminal object $*_{\set}$ in $\set$ to the terminal object $*_{\Cat}$ in $\Cat$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{ap:: def: connected category}
    A category $\cF$ is said to be \textbf{connected} if $\pi_0(\cF)$ is a singleton.
\end{definition}

\begin{cor}
If $\cF$ is connected, then for every pair of objects $x,y\in \Obj(\cF)$, there exists a sequence of morphisms $f_{i}\maps z_i \longra y_{i}$ 
such that $z_0 = x$, $z_n = y$, $\mathsf{source}(f_{i}) = \mathsf{source}(f_{i+1})$ or $\mathsf{target}(f_{i+1})$, for every $i=0,n-1$.
\end{cor}

\begin{definition}
    Let $\cF \maps \cC \ra \cD$ and $\cG\maps \cE \ra \cD$ be functors to a common source category and assume that $\cD$ has finite limits, or even just pullbacks. We define a category $\cF \downarrow \cG$, called the \textbf{comma category of $\cF$ and $\cG$} to be the pullback (in $\Cat$) of $\cF$ and $\cG$.
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}
            {\cC \times_{\cD} \cE} && \cC \\
            \\
            \cE && \cD
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=3-1]
            \arrow["\lrcorner"{anchor=center, pos=0.125}, draw=none, from=1-1, to=3-3]
            \arrow["\cF", from=1-3, to=3-3]
            \arrow["\cG"', from=3-1, to=3-3]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    This means that the category has the following structure (up to unique equivalence of categories):
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{(Objects):} The objects are triples $(c,e,f)$ where $(c,e)\in \mathsf{Ob}(\cC)\times \mathsf{Ob}(\cE)$ and $f$ is a morphism $\cF(c)\xra{f}\cG(e)$.
        \item \textbf{(Morphisms):} A morphism from $(c,e,f)$ to $(c',e',f')$ is given by a morphism $c \ra c'\in \Hom_{\cC}(c,c')$ and a morphism $e \ra e'\in \Hom_{\cE}(e,e')$ such that the following diagram  
        \begin{equation}
            \begin{tikzcd}
            {\cF(c)} && {\cF(c')} \\
            \\
            {\cG(e)} && {\cG(e')}
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow["f"', from=1-1, to=3-1]
            \arrow["\lrcorner"{anchor=center, pos=0.125}, draw=none, from=1-1, to=3-3]
            \arrow["{f'}", from=1-3, to=3-3]
            \arrow[from=3-1, to=3-3]
        \end{tikzcd}
        \end{equation}
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The notion of a comma category is available even when the target category $\cD$ does not admit pullbacks. In which case, the definition follows the description we gave above. Comma categories are useful insofar as they generalize a great deal of interesting constructions in the category $\cat$.\footnote{More accurately, comma categories are just examples of comma objects in the 2-category of categories, functors, and natural transformations.}

\begin{definition}\label{ap:: def: cat of els}
    Let $\cC$ be a category and let $\cF\maps \cC \longra \set$ be a functor on $\cC$. There is a functor $* \longra \set$, and we call the comma category $* \downarrow \cF$, together with its forgetful map to $\cC$, the \textbf{category of elements of $\cF$}. We will refer to it as
    \begin{equation}
        \int F,
    \end{equation}
    to adhere to convention.

    \medskip

    \nid Its objects are pairs $(c,*\xra{x} \cF(c))$ which, in simplified notation amounts to a pair $(c,x\in\cF(c))$. A morphism from $(c,x)$ to $(d,y)$ is given by a morphism $f\maps c \ra d$ in $\cC$ satisfying the additional condition $\cF(f)(x) = y$.
\end{definition}



\begin{definition}\label{ap:: def: initial functor}
    A functor $K\maps \cF \longra \cD$ is said to be \textbf{initial} if for any functor $F\maps \cD \longra \cM$, the induced morphism 
    \begin{equation}
        \Limit[\cD]{F} \xra{\simeq} \Limit[\cF]{(F\circ K)}
    \end{equation}
    is an isomorphism, with both sides existing if either does. Dually, we say $K$ is a \textbf{final} functor if for any functor $F\maps \cD \longra \cM$, the induced morphism
    \begin{equation}
        \Colimit[\cF]{(F\circ K)} \xra{\simeq} \Colimit[\cD]{F}
    \end{equation}
    is an isomorphism, with both sides existing if either does.
\end{definition}

\section{Kan extensions}\label{ap:: subsec: Kan extensions}

\begin{definition}
    Let $F\maps \cF \longra \cE$ and $K\maps \cF \longra \cD$ be functors. The \textbf{right Kan extension of $F$ along $K$} is a functor
    \begin{equation}
        \rkan_K(F)\maps \cD \longra \cE
    \end{equation} together with a natural transformation $\epsilon \maps \rkan_K(F)\cdot K \Rightarrow F$
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
        \cF \&\& \cE \\
        \\
        \cD
        \arrow[""{name=0, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "F", from=1-1, to=1-3]
        \arrow["K"', from=1-1, to=3-1]
        \arrow["{\rkan_K(F)}"', curve={height=12pt}, dashed, from=3-1, to=1-3]
        \arrow["\epsilon"{description}, between={0.3}{0.7}, Rightarrow, from=3-1, to=0]
    \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation} such that for any other pair $(G\maps \cD \longra \cE,\delta\maps G\cdot K\Rightarrow F)$, $\delta$ factors uniquely through $\epsilon$ as illustrated below:
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
            \cF \&\&\& \cE \\
            \\
            \cD \& {}
            \arrow[""{name=0, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "F", from=1-1, to=1-4]
            \arrow["K"', from=1-1, to=3-1]
            \arrow[""{name=1, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "{{\rkan_{K}(F)}}"{description}, curve={height=-6pt}, dashed, from=3-1, to=1-4]
            \arrow[""{name=2, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "G"', curve={height=24pt}, from=3-1, to=1-4]
            \arrow[between={0.2}{0.6}, Rightarrow, from=2, to=1]
            \arrow[between={0.6}{0.8}, Rightarrow, from=3-1, to=0]
        \end{tikzcd} \,\, = \,\,
        \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
            \cF \&\&\& \cE \\
            \\
            \cD \& {}
            \arrow[""{name=0, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "F", from=1-1, to=1-4]
            \arrow["K"', from=1-1, to=3-1]
            \arrow["G"', curve={height=24pt}, from=3-1, to=1-4]
            \arrow["\delta"{description}, between={0.3}{0.8}, Rightarrow, from=3-2, to=0]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{ap:: thm: right Kan extension pointwise}
    A right Kan extension of $F$ along $K$ is pointwise if and only if it can be computed by 
    \begin{equation}
        \rkan_{K}(F)(d) = \Limit[\phantom{.}]\bigg(d/K \xra{\mathsf{\,\,\,forget\,\,\,}} \cF \xra{\,\,\, F\,\,\,} \cE \bigg)
    \end{equation} 
    in which case, in particular, this limit exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{observation}
    If $\cE$ is complete then the right Kan extension $\rkan_{K}(F)$ is always pointwise. Analogously for left Kan extensions if $\cE$ is cocomplete.
\end{observation}



\section{Fibrations}

Fibrations are a central element around which a lot of homotopy theory revolves. Unsurprisingly, they show up as equally useful in category theory. In this section we discuss and present some of the fundamental results we use in Chapters 4--6.


\begin{definition}
    A functor $F \maps \cE \longra \cB$ is called a \define{discrete right fibration} if for every solid commuting square
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
            {\{1\}} \&\& \cE \\
            \\
            {\{0<1\}} \&\& \cB
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=3-1]
            \arrow["F", from=1-3, to=3-3]
            \arrow["{\exists!}"{description}, dashed, from=3-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[from=3-1, to=3-3]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation} 
    there is a unique diagonal filler. In other words, if every morphism $f \maps x \ra y$ in the base, together with a choice of lift $\overline{y} \in F^{-1}(\{y\})$, there exists a \emph{unique} morphism $\overline{f}\maps \overline{x} \ra \overline{y}$ such that $F(\overline{f}) = f$.
    
    \medskip

    \nid A functor $F\maps \cE \longra \cB$ is called a \define{discrete left fibration} if $F^{\op} \maps \cE^\op \longra \cB^\op$ is a discrete right fibration — equivalently, if for every solid commuting diagram 
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
            {\{0\}} \&\& \cE \\
            \\
            {\{0<1\}} \&\& \cB
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=3-1]
            \arrow["F", from=1-3, to=3-3]
            \arrow["{\exists!}"{description}, dashed, from=3-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[from=3-1, to=3-3]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    there is a unique diagonal filler.
\end{definition}

\begin{construction}\label{ap:: cons: discrete fibration}
    Given a discrete right fibration $F \maps \cE \longra \cB$ we can associate to it a a presheaf 
    \begin{equation}
        \mathsf{Un}(F) \maps \cB^{\op} \longra \set
    \end{equation} 
    defined as follows: to an object $b$ we assign the set of objects 
    \begin{equation}
       \mathsf{Un}(F)x =  \{\overline{x}\in \mathsf{Ob}(\cB):\, F(\overline{x})= x\};
    \end{equation}
    to a morphism $f\maps x \ra y$, we assign the following function 
    \begin{align}
        \mathsf{Un}(f)y &\longra \mathsf{Un}(f)x \\
                    \overline{y} &\longmapsto \mathsf{source}(\overline{g})
    \end{align}
    which to every lift of the target of $g$, assigns the unique lift of its source $x$. We will call this construction \define{unstraightening}.

    \medskip 

    \nid A more geometric interpretation of this construction is to think this presheaf as a ``sheaf of sections'' of $F$
    \begin{equation}
        \mathsf{Un}(F) = \Fun_{/\cB}(\cB/\bullet, \cE) \maps \cB^{\op} \longra \set.
    \end{equation} 
    
    \medskip
    On the other hand, given a presheaf $\cF \maps \cB^{\op} \longra \set$, its associated category of elements \Cref{ap:: def: cat of els} and respective forgetful functor
    \begin{equation}
        \int \cF \longra \cB
    \end{equation}
    define a discrete right fibration over $\cB$. Note that, given a morphism $g\maps x \ra y$, a lift of the forgetful map consists of a \emph{section} $(y, s) \in \cF(y)$ and the unique lift of $g$ is the unique morphism $(y,s) \xra{\cF(g)} (F(g)(s), x)$.
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}
    There is an equivalence of categories 
    \begin{equation}
        \int \maps \mathsf{dRFib}_{\cB} \,\, \simeq \,\, \Fun(\cB^\op, \set) \maps \mathsf{Un}.
    \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{observation}
    Under the above equivalence of categories, the representable presheaf $\cB(\bullet, b)$  corresponds to the forgetful functor $\cB/b \longra \cB$.
\end{observation}

\nid This is just the beginning of a much richer story about fibrations. Fibrations are a major topic in the theory of $(\oo,1)$-categories. For awesome surveys on the topic \cite{AF4},\cite{}

\begin{warning}
    In the next definition we will assume that by an $(\oo,1)$-category we mean a quasicategory, i.e., a simplicial set $\cC$ whose unique map to $\Delta^0$ is an inner fibration of simplicial sets.
\end{warning}

\begin{definition}
    Let $F\maps \cE \longra \cC$ be a functor of $(\oo,1)$-categories. We say that $F$ is a \define{right fibration} if for ever solid commuting diagram
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}
            {\Lambda_{i}^{p}} && \cE \\
            \\
            {\Delta^p} && \cC
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[from=1-1, to=3-1]
            \arrow[from=1-3, to=3-3]
            \arrow[dashed, from=3-1, to=1-3]
            \arrow[from=3-1, to=3-3]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    admits a lift for every $0<i\leq p$. By considering the maximal underlying Kan complex of the simplicial set $\fun_{/\cC}(\cE, \cE')$ of functors over $\cC$, we have a $\mathsf{Kan}$-enriched category 
    \begin{equation}
        \rfib_{\cC},
    \end{equation}
    which we might as well regard as an $(\oo,1)$-category.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{ap:: thm: straightening unstraightening}
    There is an equivalence of $(\oo,1)$-categories 
    \begin{equation}
        \mathsf{RFib}_{\cC} \,\, \simeq \,\, \fun(\cC^{\op}, \mathsf{Kan}),
    \end{equation}
    as implemented by straightening-unsstraightening.
\end{theorem}

\begin{observation}
Here $\mathsf{Kan}$ is a stand-in for the $(\oo,1)$-category of spaces.
\end{observation}

\begin{cor}
 There is an equivalence of $(\oo,1)$-categories 
    \begin{equation}
        d\mathsf{RFib}_{\cC} \,\, \simeq \,\, \fun(\cC^{\op}, \set),
    \end{equation}
    between right fibrations over $\cC$ with discrete fibers and $\set$-valued presheaves on $\cC$.
\end{cor}




\section{Differential topology}

This section contains a somewhat incoherent assortment of facts from differential topology, for which the reader did not find a place in the main text. They mostly revolve around smooth fiber bundles, their basic properties, and Ehresmann's fibration theorem.


\begin{definition}\label{ap:: def: smooth fibration}
    Let $\pi \maps E \longra B$ be a smooth map. We say that $f$ is a \define{locally trivial fibration} or a \textbf{smooth fiber bundle} if for every $x\in B$ there exists an open neighborhood $x\in U$ and a smooth manifold $F$, together with a fiberwise trivialization $\varphi_{U}\maps \pi^{-1}(U) \xra{\,\,\cong \,\,} U \times F$.  
\end{definition}
We do not attribute the next proposition to any single author, although details for its proof can be found in \cite[Corollary~10.3]{Hu94}.
\begin{proposition}\label{ap:: prop: triviality contractibility}
    Let $\pi\maps E \longra B$ be a smooth fiber bundle with fiber $F$, and let $B$ be a contractible smooth manifold. There exists a fiberwise diffeomorphism \[\varphi \maps F \times B \xra{\,\, \simeq \,\,} E\] so $\pi$ is isomorphic to the trivial bundle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}\label{ap:: def: proper map}
    Let $f\maps M \longra N$ be smooth map. We say $f$ is proper if $f^{-1}(K)\subseteq M$ is a compact subset of $M$ if $K\subseteq N$ is compact. 
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Ehresemann fibration lemma]\label{ap:: prop: Ehresmann fibration no boundary}\cite{Ehr50}
Let $f\maps M \longra N$ be a smooth proper submersion between smooth manifolds without boundary, then $f$ is a smooth fiber bundle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{cor}
    Let $f\maps M \longra N$ be a smooth proper submersion, then any two fibers \[f^{-1}(n) \, \simeq \, f^{-1}(m),\]
    are diffeomorphic.
\end{cor}
\begin{cor}
    Let $f\maps M \longra \RR$ be a smooth proper map. Then, for every open interval $(a,b) \subset \RR$ such that $f$ is regular in $(a,b)$. Then we have that 
    \begin{equation}
        f^{-1}\left((a,b)\right) \, \simeq \, (a,b) \times f^{-1}(c),
    \end{equation}
    for all $c \in (a,b)$. 
\end{cor}

\begin{remark}
    One might wonder whether the condition of properness is mandatory to ensure the validity of~\Cref{ap:: prop: Ehresmann fibration no boundary}. If $M$ is comapct, then $f$ being proper is automatic, but if $M$ is not compact, then the projection \[\pi_1 \maps [-1,1]\times \RR\setminus \{(0,0)\} \longra \RR\] presents a counterexample: projections are always submersions. On the other hand, it is not a smooth fiber bundle — the fiber over $0$ is $\pi^{-1}(0) \simeq [-1,0) \sqcup (0,1]$ whilst $\pi^{-1}(x) \simeq [-1,1]$ for every $x\neq 0$. The map $f$ is not compact since over $0$ the fiber is open. 
\end{remark}

For the applications we have in mind, we need to consider versions of the Ehresmann fibration lemma with boundary (and corners).

\begin{proposition}\label{ap:: prop: ehresmann boundary}
    Let $M$ and $N$ be a pair of smooth manifolds with boundary. Let $f\maps M \longra N$ be a smooth map satisfying the following additional conditions:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \label{it: prop sub} $f$ is a proper submersion.
        \item \label{it: boundary} $f_{|\partial M} \maps \partial M \longra N$ factors through $\partial N \hookra N$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Then, $f$ is a locally trivial fibration with fiber a smooth manifold wihtout boundary.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    The proof follows directly from the proof of \Cref{ap:: prop: Ehresmann fibration no boundary} adapting it only to include opens covering $\partial N$. Namely, for points $n \in \partial N$ in the boundary of the base manifold, charts are modelled after $\RR_{\geq 0} \times U$ where $U$ is an open subset of $\RR^{n-1}$. For such charts, pick an inward-pointing vector field $\partial_r$ so that flows remain inside $N$. Such a vector field can be lifted to $M$ into one which is also inward-pointing — this is ensured by condition~(\ref{it: boundary}). Therefore, this lifted inward-pointing vector field can be used to produce a trivialization near the boundary $\partial N$.
\end{proof}
