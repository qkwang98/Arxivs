With the goal of initiating a rigorous study of derived defect topological quantum field theories, this thesis constructs an $(\oo,1)$-category of stratified bordisms and sketches its $(\oo,d)$-dimensional analogue.

The first part develops a method for constructing symmetric monoidal $(\oo,1)$-categories, inspired by Segal spaces but better suited to geometric input: interval Segal spaces. Using this framework, we construct the $(\oo,1)$-category of $d$-dimensional smooth manifolds via smooth $\oo$-sheaves of manifolds, correcting a mistake in earlier work of Calaque--Scheimbauer. We then equip this category with a symmetric monoidal structure, compute its homotopy 1-category, and extend the construction to manifolds endowed with tangential structures, obtaining the interval Segal space of $d$-dimensional bordisms with tangential structure.

To model the stratifications supporting topological defects, we work with conically smooth stratified spaces, a suitably smooth framework for conical stratifications. This choice is motivated by two intertwined requirements: the ability to handle families of stratifications and the flexibility to impose tangential structures on stratified spaces.

We define stratified topological manifolds as stratified $\cB$-manifolds for a suitable $(\oo,1)$-category of basics $\cB$, that is, a sieve of singularity types. Using these, we construct an interval Segal space $\tilbordstr_d$ whose objects are compact closed stratified topological $(d-1)$-manifolds and whose 1-morphisms are stratified $d$-dimensional bordisms, and we endow it with a symmetric monoidal structure. We also use tangential structures on conically smooth stratified spaces to formulate a notion of defect labels, aiming toward bordisms with labeled defects in the style of Carqueville et al.

Finally, we sketch a higher-dimensional generalization of $\Int$ to $\cube_d$ for $d>1$, conjecturally presenting $\Cat_{(\oo,d)}$, and outline how stratified factorization homology can be used to produce derived stratified defect TQFTs.
