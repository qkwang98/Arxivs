\begin{abstract}
	In a recent paper, Tobias Fritz and the second author identified  $\sigma C^*$-algebras as a tentative noncommutative analogue of measurable spaces. 
	We advance this analogy by establishing monadicity of $\sigma C^*$-algebras over $C^*$-algebras and proving several results of interest to both the category theory and operator algebra communities.
	In detail, we show that:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item The associated probabilistic morphisms, $\sigma$-cpu maps, admit a description via a comonad closely related to the Giry monad.
		      Equivalently, $\sigma$-cpu maps can be characterized by a universal property.

		\item The category of $\sigma C^*$-algebras and $\sigma$-homomorphisms is locally $\aleph_1$-presentable.

		\item $\sigma C^*$-algebras have a tensor product induced by the maximal tensor product of $C^*$-algebras and satisfying an analogous universal property.

		\item The notion of CP-instruments, defined as $\sigma$-additive assignments from a $\sigma$-algebra to a space of completely positive maps, can be adapted to this setting by taking as target the set of $\sigma$-normal completely positive maps between $\sigma C^*$-algebras. This yields a bijective correspondence with $\sigma$-cpu maps out of the tensor product of the previous item.
	\end{itemize}
\end{abstract}
